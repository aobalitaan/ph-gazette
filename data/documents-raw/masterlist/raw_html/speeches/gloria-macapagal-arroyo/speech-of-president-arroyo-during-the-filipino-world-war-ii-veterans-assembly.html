Speech of President Arroyo during the Filipino World War II Veterans Assembly

September 3, 2009

Speech
of
Her Excellency Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo
President of the Philippines
During the Filipino World War II Veterans Assembly

[Delivered at Camp Aguinaldo, September 3, 2009]

Thank you.

Thank you Secretary Teodoro for your introduction. And thank you also for helping me fight for the cause of the veterans in the United States.

Undersecretary Carolina, President of the Veterans Federation Colonel de Ocampo, Chairman Diaz of the Veterans Affairs Committee. And we have here Congresswoman Roman and her husband Antonino Roman, Congresswoman Herminia Roman and her husband former Congressman Roman, and Congressman Roman Romulo — panay Roman tayo dito ah — and Congressman Dodo Mandanas. General Ibrado and the other members of the Armed Forces, our dear veterans, our sons and daughters of the veterans, ladies and gentlemen, good afternoon.

Una sa lahat, binabati ko ang mga beteranong narito ngayon. Kasi gusto kong ulitin na kinikilala ng buong sambayanan na malaking karangalan ang inyong binigay sa ating bansa. Kaya karangalan ko ngayon na makasama kayo, kayo ang mga bayaning nagtanggol sa kalayaan ng ating bayan at ng buong mundo. Dahil sa matinding pagtatanggol ninyo sa Bataan at iba pang bahangi ng Pilipinas nung yung mga kalaban ay pumunta rito, natagalan yung giyera dito hindi nakapunta sa ibang mga lugar na pupuntahan pa nila pagkatapos, kaya you saved not only the Philippines but the whole world.

At naalala ko kayo kahit na nung ako ay nasa Africa kung saan ako nanggaling, why? Because Africa today means so much to our peace and stability. The peace and stability of Africa means so much to our energy security, but more importantly, the safety and welfare of many of our overseas Filipinos, including our seafarers.

And on our part, like you during the Second World War contributing to the safety of the whole world, we also are an important contributor to peace and stability on the African continent. Because we have hundreds of Filipino soldiers and policemen in different United Nations missions in Africa including Darfur in Sudan and Cote d’ Ivoire. And these were very important conflicts that were discussed during the African Summit on Conflict Resolution that I just attended. Our soldiers and policemen in Africa are helping to preserve peace and they are a tribute to the professionalism and dedication of our men and women in uniform. They are our nation’s investment in global peace and they are your heirs, your spiritual heirs, they continue your tradition of courage for the cause of freedom.

And I know that their sacrifice in Africa is not a sacrifice joyfully borne. It is work where it can be found in faraway places, among strangers with different cultures and it is very lonely to be there in those distant outpost of the world. Kaya nagsisikap tayong lumikha dito sa atin ng mga trabahong maganda so that overseas work will just be a career option and not the only option for a Filipino.

And one of the things we try to do to be able to create good jobs here in the Philippines is to develop our PHIVIDEC Industrial Estate. So, for instance, I was asking Colonel de Ocampo, how is PHIVIDEC of which he is the chairman representing the veterans? And he said it’s okay, doing fine. Thanks to the support of the national government and we are hoping that very soon — actually, they’ve already started to put up a training center. Hanjin which has a major project, a billion-dollar project in Subic and Bataan and Zambales will do its even bigger billion-dollar plus expansion in the PHIVIDEC area of Misamis Oriental.

Hindi ako nag-aatubiling bisitahin ang ating taong-bayan at kanilang mga hosts sa buong mundo mula Africa hanggang American Midwest — ngayon ang hometown ni President Obama — nakikinig sa mga problema ng ating mga Pilipino, mga pangangailangan ng ating mga Pilipino doon at inaalam kung papano sila matutulungan ng ating pamahalaan by working out better policies on migrant labor, for instance.

Halimbawa sa Libya, iniwanan ko doon nung ako ay umuwi kagabi si Secretary Nitoy Roque para mabuo niya yung kanyang labor agreement doon sa Minsitry of Labor ng Libya. Another thing we do is to save lives and restore liberty. Yung mga ating mga manggagawa, kasi kung by the law of average if we have eight million people abroad siguro naman may ilang magkakaroon ng problema sa batas at merong mga bansa na may death penalty. Yun ay malaking napapakiusap natin na kung hindi tayo pumupunta doon at personal na humihingi ng pabor sa mga pinuno nila ay marami doon ay nagkaroon na ng kanilang execution ng death penalty.

Our vigorous international engagement has also brought in foreign investments. Kaya tignan naman ninyo itong mga investments na pumasok sa ating administrasyon, 15 times over what we started with at kasama ng ating mga overseas Filipinos, itong mga investments natin ay nagdoble tuloy ang ating foreign exchange at pinalakas ang ating piso at naiiwasan ang lubhang pagtaas ng presyo. In fact, our inflation rate is the lowest today in many many years. At sa gitna ng ating global recession na upgrade pa ang ating credit rating. Kaya habang yung ating mga kapaligirang bansa ay bumababa ang kanilang foreign exchange reserves over the past years, yung atin naman ay lumalaki by three billion dollars. At kahit na two-thirds ng mundo ay nasa recession during the last twelve months, tayo 34 quarters of uninterrupted growth. Yun ang ating nararanasan sa Pilipinas.

Pero yung global engagement natin ay hindi lamang para sa kapayapaan, hindi lamang para sa investment, it has also corrected historical injustice. Gaya nga ng sabi ni Colonel de Ocampo hindi pa ako pangulo ay pag pumupunta ako sa America parating bahagi ng aking agenda ay yung veterans rights from the beginning. And I was so happy that last February, the day that we visited Washington, Senator Daniel Inouye, Chairman of the Senate Committee on Appropriations sponsored the benefits for our veterans as part of American’s stimulus package.

Mabuti sinabi nga ni Undersecretary Carolina nandiyaan yung language nung nasa batas mismo na provision that recognizes our veterans. Siguro para meron siyang kapareha, I will also give you what Senator Inouye gave me what he said when he defended the veterans provisions. But let me tell you what he said verbally first. He said: “On January 26, 1941, the President of the U.S., Mr. Roosevelt, issued a military order through General MacArthur calling upon Filipinos to volunteer in the Army to serve in the Navy, to serve in the Air Force, because the President sensed correctly that there was much instability and violence in Asia. He felt the time had come for the U.S. to be prepared for any eventuality. As a result of that call, 470,000 Filipinos stepped forward and volunteered to serve in the military under the command of General MacArthur.”

As we all know, on December 7, 1941, Pearl Harbor was bombed, and then the forces of Japan began advancing to the Philippines. The first major target was the Bataan Peninsula. The 14th Japanese Army surrounded the peninsula. That peninsula contained at the moment 80,000 troops. We all assumed that the 80,000 were American troops, sabi niya. No, about 18,000 were American troops; the rest were Filipinos. Yes, the majority of the troops in Bataan were Filipinos, but somehow, sabi niya, if you look at Hollywood on the Bataan Death March, you will hardly see a Filipino marching. So kaya siguro hindi kinikilala ang ginawa ng mga Pilipino nung panahong yun. In fact, sabi niya sa akin sa ibang mga conversation, ang nakikita nila doon si Audie Murphy pero hindi yung ating mga magigiting na Pilipino.

But anyway to continue what he said on the Senate floor: “Of the survivors of Bataan, 15,000 were Americans, 60,000 were Filipinos. The march took a little over a month. They were not given medicine or water. But the time it ended, 54,000 survived. Very few Filipinos survived. And then we had Corredigor. The same thing.”

In March 1942, the Congress of the U.S. — the Senate and the House of the U.S.– passed a measure thanking the Filipinos for their gallantry, for their heroism, and said: “If you wish, you may become a citizen of the U.S. and get all the benefits.” But the war ended, and in February of 1946, the U.S. Congress passed a bill rescinding, repealing the Act of 1942. Sabi nga ni Senator Inouye, believe it or not, Congress declared that the service the Filipinos had rendered was not active duty. So yun ang malaking insulto na nangyari sa ating mga beterano sa ating bansa.

And to go back to Senator Inouye, and he speaks for all of us when he said, we waited all this time. We have had measure after measure presented. We did so in the proper fashion but we got filibustered, we got ruled out and everything else. Yun ang sabi niya.

Well on my part, as I said, in my visits to the U.S. even before I became president and especially since 2001, I have been fighting for our veterans cause, for our veterans benefits. Naalala ko yung dating U.S. Secretary of Veterans Affairs ay talagang nahihirapan na siya sa akin kasi tuwing pumupunta ako doon I would call him to come and see me and we talk about this bill. Hirap na hirap siyang maipasa sabi niya, pero nagbigay sila ng mga interim measures. Kasama sa interim measures na binigay nila, the U.S. has been equipping our Veterans Hospital since my state visit of 2003 in America, another direct benefit of our engagement with the United States. In any case, after the sponsorship speech of Senator Inouye, on that day that we were in Washington, the U.S. recognized you our Filipino World War II veterans.

So last July, I accepted the invitation of President Obama to be the first Southeast Asian leader to meet him at the White House, partly in order to express our appreciation for recognizing our veterans in their fiscal stimulus package. And I told him that this long overdue recognition will hopefully go a long way toward rewarding you, our patriotic Filipinos who fought side-by-side with Americans under U.S. command during those heroic days.

I also thanked him and the U.S. government for equipping our military and training them to enhance their professionalism and effectiveness, and to advance “soft” power by helping build schools, roads, bridges and building our local economy through American development assistance in Mindanao and across the nation. These joint efforts have helped strengthen our paradigm for peace in Mindanao.

Andaming naitutulong ng mga Amerikano sa ating laban kontra sa terorismo lalo na tuwing nagkakaron ng meeting ang inyong lingkod at ang Pangulo mas gumaganda ang tulong. Halimbawa ngayon meron tayong mga kaso kontra sa mga Abu Sayyaf, yung ating mga prosecutors ay tinutulungan ng American forces sa kanilang safety, sa kanilang transportation at lahat-lahat kaya gumaganda ang prosecution. Kasi nung araw halos walang gustong pumunta sa Sulu magprosecute dahil natatakot ngunit dahil tinutulungan sila sa mga facilities, while Americans cannot find on the ground, they can help in so many other ways. So those are some of the ways that many don’t know about by which they help our fight against terrorism.

Nung ako ay pumunta sa Washington kasama ko si Gilbert, Senators Inouye and Daniel Akaka, who is Chairman of the U.S. Veterans Affairs Committee of the Senate, visited us at the Presidential Suite to receive also our thanks for being most instrumental in having the veterans recognition included in the stimulus package.

Naalala ko nung hindi pa Democrats yung nanduon sa majority ng Senado at Congress, at Senator Inouye was the ranking minority member of the Senate Committee on Appropriations, naalala ko sinabi niya sa akin minsan dahil parati tayo nakikipag-ugnayan sa kanya dahil siya ang champion ng mga beterano sa package veterans compensation. Sabi niya sa akin, hayaan mo at pag bumalik na yung mga kapartido ko at magiging majority kami muli ako muli ang magiging chairman ng Committee on Appropriations at titiyakin ko magbibigay tayo ng karapatan sa ating mga Filipino veterans, at yun na nga ang nangyari.

I also met with the Philippine Friendship Caucus in the U.S. House of Representatives to thank them for all the years they have fought for our veterans benefits. Yung mga yun parati kong binibisita sa Kongreso para palakasin ang kanilang loob so that they will continue to fight for us, kasi remember yung mga lumalaban para sa atin ay yung mga kung saan maraming Filipino. Halimbawa San Diego, yung mga congressmen ng San Diego talagang pinaglalaban nila ang ating veterans benefits. Kaya lang meron din naman yung mga congressmen doon sa mga lugar na walang masyadong Filipino nagiging very protectionist sila ngayon doon sa recession ng America. Sila naman ay lumalaban, sabi nila, bakit mo bibigyan ng pera yung mga nandoon sa ibang bansa. Eh kaya nga natin dito kaya kailangan pinapalakas natin ang loob ng ating mga kaibigang congressman doon and that is what we have been doing. Last February, we did that and now this July, we thanked them.

At binanggit ng mga senador at kongresista, nang kami ni Gilbert ay nagpasalamat sa kanila, na yung lump sum payment na naisama doon sa stimulus package ay bukod pa sa mga benepisyong dating binibigay sa inyo. Sabi nga nila: “Please make sure to remind everybody that what we are providing for in the stimulus package is in addition to the present benefits they’re already getting, not as a replacement of the present benefits.”

Pinapaalala nila, in addition to the recognition of your military service and your lump sum payments, those enjoying the supplementary security income will continue with it. For instance, si U.S. Secretary of Veterans Affairs, si Eric Shinseki binisita rin kami ni Gilbert sa Presidential Suite. He pointed out that outside of the new veterans… when he called on us during our Washington visit that outside of the new veterans benefits his department has also been disbursing funds to veterans and their families, including death pensions in the amount of 17 million dollars a month. Sabi nga ni Kristie Kenney 12 million dollars, ang nag correct si Congressman Mandanas, sabi niya: “No, it’s not 12 million dollars, it’s 17 million dollars.” So yung buong sum na yun, the aggregate sum is even bigger than the new stimulus package.

They’re cumulatively more than what the stimulus package provides for. And he also assured me that the assistance to the Veterans Hospital would continue. He reported that the U.S. is providing an MRI facility to the veterans hospital. Tapos siguro I hope you don’t mind sinabi ko rin sa kanya, sana sa mga darating if you can help put up facilities for veterans in the provinces where a lot of them live then that would be very helpful kasi matatanda na ang mga veterans. Sana hindi na kailangang magbiyahe pa sa Pilipinas para magkaroon ng treatment.

Alam ko kasi… alam na natin kasi ngayon kung saan nakatira yung mga nakararaming beterano dahil nung nilagda ni President Obama ang mga benepisyo, agad tayong kumilos upang masimulan ang proseso ng application at pagsumite ng dokumento, at kabilang sa direktiba ko tungkol dito ang pagpapatayo ng mga processing center sa mga probinsiya kung saan marami ang mga beteranong nakatira kaya alam na natin ngayon where the veterans live. And therefore I hope and I will ask Secretary Teodoro to make sure that that is carried out, that future facilities will not only be in the Veterans Hospital but in areas around the Philippines where we have many many veterans living there.

Nung ako ay magmimiting na kay Secretary Shinseki maraming mga beteranong nag text sa akin — although they went through friends whom they know have my text number — nag text sa akin, and their message was: that they look forward to the speedy implementation of the provisions for Filipino veterans. At yun ang sinabi ko kay Secretary Shinseki na sana hanapan ng paraan kung papano mas mabilis pa ang pagproseso ng veterans benefits. And he replied that his Department if needed would hire additional personnel to fast track the claims. When I apprised her, so ito kailangan din i-follow up nila Gilbert yung pangako ni Secretary Shinseki, na paparamihin nila yung mga empleyado nila na nagpoproseso ng claims para mas mabilis asikasuhin yung claims ng ating mga beterano.

When I apprised Senators Inouye and Akaka of the developments in the processing of the claims, he asked whether the funds for the processing of claims would be sufficient. And Secretary Teodoro reported that more than 31,000 claims have already been filed with the PVAO headed by Nessie Carolina, whereas the original estimate was only 18,000, so halos doble ang nag file compared to the estimate. So that is one of the problems, so now Senators Inouye and Akaka know about that. The first payment if you remember was issued last April 8. Now we expect hopefully the filing of claims to go faster now that Secretary Shinseki made his commitment and Secretary Teodoro will follow up on that commitment.

We must beat the February 16 deadline next year for the filing of all claims. And that is what I’m glad to be able to have the chance to meet with you about, to remind everyone, tell all your members they have to file the claims before the 16th of February.

You know Colonel de Ocampo was talking about our efforts for the veterans benefits. Kung hindi natin ginawa itong efforts baka hanggang ngayon wala pa ito. And therefore I was very honored, I am very honored that it was during our administration now that the U.S. Congress and the U.S. administration recognized more than 60 years of struggle endured by the surviving Filipino World War II veterans. I am also honored that it was under our administration that the veterans of WWII as well as of other wars finally received your old age pensions that were not paid by previous administrations.

Nung ako ay pumasok bilang pangulo nakita ko yung arears, seven billion pesos, eh budget deficit na, malaking budget deficit natin nuon parang imposible na mababayaran natin itong utang na ito, but now we have paid them. And that is thanks also to our congressmen, they can always put a provision paying you, but if they don’t help you with the tax measures we won’t be able to pay you. But they have helped us with the tax measures, with the Value Added Tax, kaya nabayaran ang mga utang ng old age pensions ng ating beterano.

Now, that we paid the old age pensions, the arrears, we go to looking at the other debts that the national government has incurred because of unfunded legislation. Ang isa dito yung total administrative liability, ang laki-laki nang utang na yun. Kaya kausap ko si Nonoy Andaya kagabi at hanggang kaninang umaga, hanggang ngayon dito nung nakikita niyong nagtetext ako, nagcecellphone ako, si Andaya ang kausap ko. Papano natin masisimulaan na bayaran ito? Sabi ko kay Nonoy, at least bayaran natin yung total administrative liability ng World War II veterans for the year 2009 at doon tayo magsisimula.

Kaya magtulungan tayo lahat para maipaabot sa mga beterano natin, ang ating mga bayani ng kahapon, na dapat ituloy ang paggalang hanggang ngayon ang mga beterano na tulungan sa mga benepisyong nararapat.

Sa inyong lahat, maraming salamat sa pagkakataon maglingkod sa inyo. Thank you.
