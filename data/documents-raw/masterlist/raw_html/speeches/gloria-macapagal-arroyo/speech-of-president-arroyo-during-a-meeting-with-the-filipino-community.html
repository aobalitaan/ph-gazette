Speech of President Arroyo during a meeting with the Filipino community

November 13, 2009

Speech
of
Her Excellency Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo
President of the Philippines
During a meeting with the Filipino community

[Delivered at the Grand Ballroom, The Fullerton Hotel, Singapore, November 13, 2009]

Thank you. Maraming salamat.

Maraming salamat. Thank you.

Thank you Secretary Romulo for your introduction.

Thank you Ambassador Mindy Cruz for your very, very eloquent warm words of welcome and tribute to our quality Pinoys here in Singapore.

I’d like to introduce also the members of our delegation who are here on the head table. If I may go from my left to my right: Our Press Secretary is Cerge Remonde, gikan sa Cebu; our congresswoman from the BUHAY Partylist, Iyay Coscolluela; our Secretary of Finance, Secretary Gary Teves; our congresswoman from Pangasinan, Congresswoman Rachel Arenas; and then next to Secretary Romulo, our Deputy Speaker of the House of Representatives, Girlie Villarosa; ing congresswoman kong kabalen, si Congresswoman Anna Bondoc; our congresswoman from Manila, Naida Angping; and our Secretary of Trade and Industry, Peter Favila.

At naman sa inyong lahat, magandang gabi, mayap a bengi king kabalen kong Kapampangan. Dagiti kakailyak ti bangir ni mamang ko, naimbag nga rabii yo amin. San saray kabaleyan, masantos ya labi ed sikayon amin. Sa mga taga-Visayas ug Mindanao, maayong gabii kaninyong tanan.

Maraming, maraming salamat sa muli ninyong mainit na pagsalubong, na kasing-init ng pagbati ninyo noong 2007.

Natutuwa ako at muli tayong nagkaroon ng pagkakataon na magkasama muli, na ako’y nagkaroon ng pagkakataon na makadalaw muli sa inyo at makadalaw dito. Actually, Dr. Reyes mentioned the times that I have visited, I visited one or two more times but I come here for lunch, for a business lunch and then I leave, so hindi ko kayo nakikita sa mga ganung panahon. Pero ngayon, masarap naman at nagkasama tayo at ako naman ay masayang-masaya dito sa ating “Salute to the Filipinos in Singapore.” Thank you for giving me the opportunity to join in this Salute.

At talaga naman, balitang-balita yung kabutihang ginagawa ng mga Pilipino dito, at ang laki ng inyong kontribusyon sa ekonomiya ng Singapore, ang laki din ng inyong kontribusyon sa inyong mga pamilya at sa ating mga kababayan sa Pilipinas. Kaya kapuri-puri ang lahat ng ginagawa ninyo.

What you have shown here, totoo yun. Kilalang-kilala ang taga-rito. You are hardworking, you are talented, you are truly quality Filipinos. Nakita natin sa diskurso ni Mindy, ni Dr. Reyes, sa ating video yung iba’t-ibang klase ng manggagawang Pilipinong nagtratrabaho dito. Siyempre, yung unang nakararaming Pilipino na nagpunta rito in the earlier — maybe like ten, fifteen years ago — yung mga kasambahay. Ngayon, nakita natin may mga Pilipinos nasa industriya ng transportasyon kasi dinig ko kay Mindy, bahagi doon sa mga gumagawa ng inyong train system na pinagmamalaki ng mga Singaporeans ay mga Pilipino.

Sa IT, yun na nga yung isa sa mga kasunduan natin nung nag-state visit ako ng 2001, at naalala ko noong nagpunta ako rito in one of my visits and we had a chance to meet with a smaller group of the Filipino Community — one table only — grabe yung mga IT talents dito, ang babata. Noong bumalik ako sa Pilipinas, sabi ko doon sa aming ICT, at that time, Private-Public Sector Task Force, naku! ang tatanda na natin, yung mga genius doon ng IT na Pilipino, who are earning so well, they’re all so young, they are all young enough to be our children, talagang bagong henerasyon, quality Filipinos and I’m sure that there’s more of you in the generations to come.

Yung mga bangko, nabanggit nga si Lito Camacho at saka si Ding de Guzman. Yung mga ospital, naku! di lang nurse at doctor, noong nagkaroon ng SARS dito sa Singapore, yung mga nurse kasama sa mga bayani ng Singapore na pinagmamalaki ng Pilipino.

At pati nga yung mga kasambahay, mas mahusay ngayon, piling-pili at karamihan dumadaan pa sa training. At I’m so happy that that Bayanihan Center that we worked hard for — I remember even when I was still Vice President asking the Singapore government na sana ilibre yung lupa tapos tinayo ng mga Filipino community dito, sila Ding de Guzman very active doon — I’m so happy to see in the video na napakasagisag pa ang kanyang paglilingkod sa ating mga Pilipino dito.

And as we have seen our OFWs in Singapore have moved up the value chain to higher-paying jobs that require more complex skills. We have seen that Filipinos here excel in various fields, yun na nga si Rico Hizon kasama doon, si Vice President Bonilla ng Citibank.

The OFW community over the years has experienced such a significant improvement in your quality of life as well. Even the once who used to earn less are earning more because they are more skilled, and of course, first and foremost in importance, you continue to uphold the reputation of Filipinos as excellent and dedicated workers. Congratulations!

Natutuwa rin ako na nakikilala na ngayon ang kulturang Pilipino dito sa Singapore dahil sa inyong pagpupunyagi, dahil kilala kayo, dahil matagumpay kayo dito, nakakatulong kayo na magdala at magpakita ng kultura ng Pilipinas. On this 40th Anniversary of our Diplomatic Relations, one of the… no! not one but three or even four of the exhibits are happening right now about Philippine Culture in three different places because we came from the other two exhibits and it’s so good to know that the Singaporeans are getting to know more about our culture. One of the exhibits is dedicated to the fiesta, kaya kilalang-kilala, nakikilala ang fiesta at iba’t-ibang mga kultura at kaugalian ng iba’t-bang mga rehiyon ng Pilipinas.

Early this evening, we were with Lito Camacho, one of the sponsors of the exhibit of Philippine Art at the Singapore Art Museum. And I am so happy and I encourage everyone to support these events. Maybe you can invite your Singaporean and other friends to see and experience the wealth of our culture.

As you know, I will attend the APEC Leaders’ Economic Meeting here in Singapore starting tomorrow. The theme of APEC 2009 is “Sustaining Growth, Connecting the Region.” We will discuss four priority areas:

First, addressing the crisis and preparing for recovery; Second, supporting the multilateral trading system; Third, accelerating regional integration; and Fourth, strengthening APEC.

Yung una nga, yung pinag-uusapan, addressing the crisis, preparing for the recovery, yun naman talaga ay isang napakaimportanteng pag-uusapan dapat.

Noong unang tumama ang global economic crisis, maraming mga ekonomista ay nangamba na baka tayo sa Pilipinas ay babagsak din. At isang dahilan sabi nila na baka babagsak tayo ay baka raw bumaba ang mga remittances ng OFW dahil marami sa mga OFW ay nagtratrabaho sa mga bansa na naapektuhan ng krisis. Halimbawa nga dito sa Singapore, ngunit sa kabila ng maraming mga Pilipino ay nagtratrabaho sa maraming mga bansa na natamaan ng krisis, pinakita ninyo na hindi ninyo pinapabayaan, hindi pinapabayaan ng Pilipino ang kanyang pamilya at kanyang bansa, kahit na dumanas pa sa maraming hirap. Kaya sa halip na bumaba ang remittances ay tumaas pa. And now, as of the month of August, remittances in fact reached already $11 billion dollars.

At alam ko na ito ay nanggagaling din sa mga sakripisyong pinapasan niyo sa ibang bansa upang makapagtrabaho at makapagdala sa inyong pamilya. I’m glad that there are several of you, especially the expat executives who can have your families here, pero marami din ay hindi puwedeng magdala ng kanilang pamilya. Yun ay malungkot na trabaho at batid ko ang hirap at lungkot na dinadanas nitong mga ganitong uri ng pagtratrabaho na hindi madadala ang pamilya dito.

And so my vision is to help make our economy competitive enough, and I’ve said this to you before, so that one day going abroad will just be a career choice, not the only option for our hardworking Filipino workers. And how to do that in this ever interconnected world? Well, the world is safer, more secure and more vibrant when leaders from so many nations come together to solve problems and look to the future together. That is the strength and the hope of the APEC meeting that will start tomorrow: to work hand in hand with our Pacific neighbors to forge a more perfect world.

Here, I know that every person has felt the devastation of the setback in the global economy that affected even such a strong economy as Singapore. At lalo na yung mga hindi naman executive ang trabaho or hindi naman IT expertise and trabaho, mahihirap na pamilyang mga pinanggalingan sa Pilipinas, yung mga kamag-anak nung mga nagtratrabaho na nanggagaling sa mga mahihirap na pamilya sa Pilipinas, their relatives back home are the most vulnerable to the global shocks; their relatives are the most price sensitive. They feel the real pain when prices surge, when jobs are lost, when food prices are out of reach.

But one of the things we can say in APEC is that at least here in Asia, we are on a solid comeback; we have weathered the storm better than other regions and with greater resilience. Even the Philippines, with the challenges of a developing nation was prepared for the global shocks. It could have been worse if we had not taken the hard and unpopular but necessary economic and fiscal reforms several years ago. Kasama nga doon si Lito Camacho when he was still Secretary of Finance at kasama rin doon yung isang dating Singapore expat din, si Vince Perez who was BOI Managing Head for a while and move to other Economic Ministries like the Department of Energy later on. These reforms have what we could say, have kept the wolf away from the door. But the wolf is still out there. Isa nga sabi nung nag-meet yung mga nagsalubong sa akin na Singaporean officials — because they had meetings of the finance ministers, isn’t it, Gary? the trade ministers — pinag-uusapan on when the fiscal stimulus should start to be taken but because all the countries did fiscal stimulus including us. And I said: no, it’s too early for an exit plan. But then yes, they all agree. Sabi ko, actually, ang isang malaking problema kasi lahat naman tayo I think agreed that it’s too early for an exit plan. It’s too early to stop the fiscal stimulus, ang problema, how do you finance the stimulus? Yun ang problema at at least masasabi ko sa Pilipinas, we can still finance the fiscal stimulus, our bonds are still very, very marketable. So, yun ang dahilan kung bakit among many other things, I believe we are resilient and we will bounce back to that growth that Dr. Reyes has talked about, was talking about when I came here in 2007 that rapid growth that we achieved before it slowed down somewhat but at least never turned negative during the global economic crisis.

Our trip to attend the APEC Summit reflects the commitment of our administration to active engagement in trade, commerce and economic cooperation among nations. This energetic engagement, for instance, my many visits to Singapore has helped us in the country lift up the poor and feed the hungry. And so, it is important that we continue to engage globally.

Now, one of the things that I would like to point out tomorrow in the Summit is that one of the major dynamics of the 21st century is likely to be not just the flow of financial capital, not just the flow of goods and services but human capital, and I say that from experience. Tignan niyo, yung ating quality Pinoys na narito. You are just the pioneers of what will become, I believe in this coming century, a real global phenomenon.

The flow of labor will determine much about how the world grows and works together. So we have had a lot of trade now in goods, we’re having a lot of trade in services like air services, for instance, telecommunications. We are also now about to enter a great age of global labor.

Today, it is the Filipinos among the leaders but tomorrow it will be many, many countries doing this. But we are pioneers here and we can tell the world that we have the chance to make sure we tap the possibilities. It is very important I believe that as we must recognize that as trade has expanded in goods, as trade has expanded in services, trade will expand as far as labor mobility is concerned. And the world might as well recognize it for one thing to make it orderly and the other one also, the more you recognize it, the more orderly you make it, the more you stop the abuse that sometimes preys on global laborers of all walks of life in economic strata.

Dito nga sa Singapore sa mga nakaraang taon, naririnig natin yung mga karanasan ng ating mga manggagawa, mga domestic helpers, di ba? nagkakaroon ng abuso kontra sa kanila but as the market becomes more sophisticated with the entry of the professionals, the CEOs, the COOs, mas napoproteksiyon ang ating mga manggagawa, lalo na yung manggagawa in the more manual kinds of work that are more vulnerable to abuse. So we need to put order there, the way that WTO was intended to put order in trade, and we need to put order also in the flow of human capital. Kasi, yun na nga, because we are pioneers in this new age of global labor, it is our obligation to make sure that we protect our workers wherever they may be.

Talagang gusto kong pasalamatan ang mga Pilipinong nagtatrabaho dito sa Singapore. Una sa lahat nga, tinaas ninyo ang antas ng mga Pilipino dito, nakakatulong sa proteksiyon ng kapwa niyong Pilipino dito. At patuloy, gaya ng kwenento niyo kanina, ang malasakit na pinapakita ninyo para sa ating bansa. Tunay kayong mga kaakibat, katuwang, kapuso sa pagkamit ng ating mga layunin sa isang magandang kinabukasan.

Ako’y nagpapasalamat doon sa nagpadala ng mga tulong dito sa ating mga nasalanta doon sa ating mga bagyo na nangyari itong nakaraang Setyembre at Oktubre. And that again shows how you Filipinos here in Singapore never forget the folks back home no matter how successful you are here. And so I am very grateful that our global Filipino, our quality Filipino — global, quality, Filipino pa rin. And you love your country and I really thank you very much and appreciate you for that.

Ipagpatuloy natin nawa ang ating pagtutulungan at pananalig sa ating mga programang pangkaunlaran, sabay-sabay nating kamtan ang magandang hinaharap para sa lahat.

I thank you very much for your words of appreciation, Dr. Reyes in behalf of the whole Filipino Community, and may I tell you that you are also very, very much appreciated not only by me but all the folks back home.

Maraming salamat. Mabuhay ang quality Pinoy ng Singapore!

Thank you.
