Speech of President Arroyo during the 4th Annual National Convention of the Philippine Schools District Supervisors Association

July 21, 2004

Speech
of
Her Excellency Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo
President of the Philippines
During the 4th Annual National Convention of the Philippine Schools District Supervisors Association

[Delivered at the Queen Margarette Hotel Ballroom, Brgy. Domoit, Lucena City, Quezon, July 21, 2004]

Maraming salamat.

Maraming salamat, Secretary De Jesus sa iyong pagpakilala sa akin. Maraming salamat din Governor Enverga sa inyong pag-welcome sa aming lahat: sa akin, kay Secretary De Jesus, sa lahat ng mga supervisors ng DepEd sa buong Pilipinas.

Salamat rin mayor talaga sa inyong pagsalubong and for hosting this important convention in your city. Congratulations din Dr. Reyes. Congratulations on being able to put this convention together and I hope that indeed this convention will recharge the batteries of all our district supervisors.

Other officials of DepEd, other officials of the Philippine Schools District Supervisors Association, other officials of Quezon including the various mayors and board members who joined me today, ladies and gentlemen:

Mga supervisors, first of all, thank you for inviting me to be with you today. I’ve been avoiding speaking engagements since the elections, but as I told Dra. Reyes, kayo importante kayo sa akin. So, you are an exception. I want to take this opportunity for one thing — to thank you for the great role you played in the last elections, especially because we’ve not yet been able to automate our elections. Once again, you have to bear the burden of delivering credible elections to our people. And for your achievement and sacrifice, thank you very much and congratulations!

I hope that when I step down six years from now, the electoral process will have been completely computerized so that you the educators of our country can concentrate exclusively on education.

And I hope that when I step down six years from now, your work will be easier because everyone of school age will be in school, in an uncrowded classroom, in surroundings conducive to learning. Hangad kong makapasok sa eskuwela ang bawat bata, may sapat na lugar sa silid-aralan, may computer sa bawat paaralan, at may sapat na suweldo ang mga guro.

As Dr. Reyes said, the salary grade of public school district supervisors has been upgraded during my last term: from salary grade 19 to 20 last 2003, and this month, from salary grade 20 to 21. Congratulations to all you! Also, aside from that the post of public school district supervisor, which used to be co-terminus, is now a permanent position.

In order to further upgrade your salary, as well as that of all the teachers and other government employees, well need a law. And I shall ask Congress — kaya sabi ko dapat inimbita yung bagong congressman ng Quezon dito eh. Yung distrito ni first district. Anyway, I will ask Congress to pass a government re-engineering law with a golden parachute to be financed on a reimbursement basis by the world bank so that early retirement will not add to the budget deficit. And then we can generate savings to increase the pay of teachers and other employees of a mean and lean bureaucracy. We will reward those who worked hard and say goodbye to those who did not work hard.

To uplift our people from poverty and to build our nation, its imperative to upgrade our standards of learning.

In the medium term, there’s a large possibility that the national economy will enter into a period of unprecendented expansion. But only if we do what we have to do. There is little time to waste. We have to quickly resolve some of the most pressing problems besetting us. We have to set ourselves to work for immediate achievement in five key areas:

The job creation and economic growth package. Kaya kanina nung nag-meeting kami nila governor at mayor bago pumunta rito, sinabi ko kay governor, I have to open two million more hectares of agri-business. Siguro sa Quezon ang dami pang lugar. Sabi nga ni governor, yung General Nakar lamang isang bayan lamang yon. Mas malaki kaysa sa buong probinsiya ng Cavite. So there is much room for expansion of agri-business and expansion of jobs here in the province of Quezon.

Another key area, second key area is the anti-corruption and good governance package; the third is the social justice and basic needs package; the fourth is the energy independence and savings package; and the fifth is the education improvement and youth opportunity package.

Let me talk about this fifth package. Let me outline the agenda of action to realize this governments commitment to improving educational services and opening new opportunities for our youth.

We need to construct at least 3,000 new classrooms each year, and grant a college or VocTech scholarship for every indigent family. We will prepare our youth to be the next generation of knowledge workers. Well upgrade math, science and english teaching in basic education. At siyempre magsisimula tayo sa mga teachers. Pagalingan ang mga teachers sa math, pagalingan ang mga teachers sa science, pagalingan ang mga teachers sa english.

We will provide all public high schools with computers. And instead of requiring a fifth year in high school, we will incorporate preschool in the education ladder. We will encourage early childhood development as a requirement for elementary education. Sabi nga ni governor, nung sinabi niya yung dami ng mga barangay day care centers dito, naiintindihan ko kung bakit nahihirapan i-incorporate yung day care centers eh. Kasi, day care centers dati-dati DSWD. Pero ngayon devolve na yon. Local governments na yon. Hindi na national DSWD. So dapat yung principal at saka yung local government magkakaugnay para yung curriculum ng day care ay kindergarten na. At saka, eh trabaho nyo ito di ba? Kasi curriculum trabaho ninyo kaya sinasabi ko ito sa inyo.

At saka naalaala ko sa aking pagbibisita-bisita sa mga probinsya, siyempre madalas yung meeting ginaganap sa eskuwelahan, yon ang pinaka-function room ng community pag-barangay level. Tapos pinapakita yung mga kailangan. Tapos, kako we need a day care center. Tapos sabi ko, then you said, sabihin ko, mayor, wala ba kayong pera para sa day care? meron ho pero walang lugar. eh, nandoon ako sa school building may schoolyard — eh, yan? Hindi eh, kasi DepEd ang may-ari. You see, it should not be that way. Even if one is local government and the other is DepEd there should be a close coordination between the two. Eh, kung kulang ang lugar ng barangay, ng barangay day care center at may lugar doon sa barangay elementary school, magbigay ng lupa para makapagtayo ng day care doon. Para ganun malapit na physically and even substantively ang day care at saka ang ating grade one. Yan ang gusto kong mangyari. Kung magagawan natin yan, by Memorandum of Understanding with the DepEd and the local government, lets do it. Kung hindi naman, hihingi ako ng batas sa ating mga legislators.

I shall also ask Congress for a law strengthening the distance learning system. Meron tayo kasing Magna Carta for teachers that doesn’t allow the DepEd secretary to require a teacher to move from one place to another without his or her consent. Ang ating teachers-students ratio sa Pilipinas 30 to one, which is very, very good. On the aggregate, we don’t have a lack of teachers — 30 to one. Ang problema may mga lugar maraming teacher, may mga lugar kulang ng teacher. Kaya lang hindi malilipat yung teacher mula sa lugar kung saan sobra hanggang sa lugar kung saan kulang dahil sa Magna Carta.

Isang option, palitan yon. Pero ewan ko kung gusto nyo yon. Yung isang option, maglagay ng distance learning para sa mga lugar kung saan kulang ang teacher yung television ang additional teacher. So that you can have learning from the main school in the Poblacion to the barangay school through information technology. Kaya tamang-tama yung sinabi ni Dr. Reyes eh, nung ginamit niya yung analogy, yung cell phone. I.T. ang kanyang analogy, I.T. ang pagsagot sa low bat ng ating mga teachers sa malalayong lugar kung saan kulang ang mga teachers.

Now in the side of youth welfare, we must sustain the relentless campaign against illegal drugs and make schools also free from gambling with the help of parents and committed institutions. All these really revolves around values. Alam nyo nung ako ay nagpunta doon sa isang kalahi — alam niyo, maraming alam niyo yung kalahi, di ba? Yung mga lugar sa mga mahihirap na lugar — papunta ako sa isang kalahi area nung panahon nung canvassing, pero sa alam ng marami sa inyo, I spent that time also going to the Carmelite convents to pray with the nuns.

So, in one of the provinces during my… I was staying with the Carmelite convent but I was going to go to a Kalahi area during that day. I was telling the nuns I am going to this town to look at the completion of the Kalahi construction — may mga day care centers, kung anu-ano — sabi nung madre, ay, I know that area. It’s very poor, maraming island barangay. Sa mga island barangay walang religion, walang natutunan na religion. Eh, ang hirap-hirap ng mga tao doon. Paglaki ng mga kababaihang bata doon nagiging household help kaya lang napapa-fire out kasi nagnanakaw, kasi wala silang religion. sabi ko, oo, nga sister eh. Pag mahirap sila manatili, papasok sa tahanan, magnanakaw. Pag meron namang napag-aralan, later on papasok sa gobyerno, magnanakaw din. Dahil walang basic values formation.

So the problem of drugs, the problem of gambling in high school, in school. The problem of stealing whether in a house or in a government office it revolves around the values you grew up with. Nung ako nag-aaral sa high school naalala ko, nagtuturo kami nang religion sa mga grade school sa public school. Kung hindi man kami magkakaron ng heresy kasi may libro eh, de-kahon yon eh. Kulang lang nila manpower. So, naunawaan ko nawala na pala yon sa ating sistema nung panahon ng martial law under the guise of separation of church and state. I do not believe that that’s a proper separation of church and state. Ang separation lang, ibig sabihin ko, huwag ka magpi-favoritism kung anong relihiyon ang magtuturo. Kasi kahit na anong relihiyon kung legitimate yung religion talagang yung values naman common. Nagkakaiba lamang on whether on Jesus Christ, on blessed virgin, on statues of saints, but values are common.

And so, I’m instructing the Department of Education to allow legitimate NGOs to conduct values formation sessions in the schools para hindi kayo mahirapan. We can allow others outside to come in for values formation. Because education is not only about making our young people competitive in the global market. It is about providing Filipinos excellence to their work, and building our nation around democratic and universal values.

That is what a good education is — competitiveness and values. That is what constitutes a good education. A good education is what every parent dreams of for his or her children. This is what I want every Filipino child to have. This is what every Filipino child must enjoy.

And you and I will help make that dream come true.

Maraming salamat, good luck! Congratulations on your convention.

Thank you.
