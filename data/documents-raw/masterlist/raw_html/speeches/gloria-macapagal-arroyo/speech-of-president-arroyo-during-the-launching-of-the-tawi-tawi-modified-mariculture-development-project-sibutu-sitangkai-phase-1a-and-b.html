Speech of President Arroyo during the launching of the Tawi-Tawi Modified Mariculture Development Project, Sibutu-Sitangkai (Phase 1A and B)

May 29, 2008

Speech
of
Her Excellency Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo
President of the Philippines
During the launching of the Tawi-Tawi Modified Mariculture Development Project, Sibutu-Sitangkai (Phase 1A and B)

[Delivered at the Sibutu Municipal Complex, Sibutu, Tawi-Tawi, May 29, 2008]

Maraming salamat Secretary, Artie Yap, na ang kanyang lola ba, lola?… great grandmother ay tiga-Sulu, Jolo, Sulu. Kaya apo ng Mindanao.

Maraming salamat Congressman Nur Jafaar dito sa napakagandang ideya ninyo para tumulong sa taumbayan ng Tawi-Tawi.

Maraming salamat Governor Sahali sa inyong pag-welcome at yung paliwanag naman ninyo tungkol sa isa pang napaka-importanteng proyekto, yung hatchery.

At gusto kong batiin ang mga opisyal ng Tawi-Tawi, mga opisyal ng Sibutu sa pangunguna ni Mayor Pajiji, mga opisyal ng Sitangkai sa pangunguna ni Mayor Ahaja, ang ating iba’t-ibang Cabinet members ay narito pati na rin ang aking pamilya. At bagamat dahil nabanggit na yung iba hindi ko na sila babanggitin. Babanggitin ko lang na kasama rin natin ang kauna-unang Armed Forces Chief of Staff na nanggagaling sa Mindanao, si General Alex Yano. (applause)

Sa inyong lahat, Assalamu alaikum Tawi-Tawi!

Binabanggit kanina ni Congressman Jaafar yung aking State of the Nation Address noong isang taon, July 27 ba yun? 23. At doon sinabi ko and I quote: “Magtatayo tayo ng mariculture o palaisdaan sa dagat sa Sibutu. Hiling ito ni Nur Jaafar.” (applause)

And here we are launching that project, and we are happy that we are in the midst of the leaders of the community. And I bring you the greetings of the dignitaries from Muslim nations who are in Bongao who would have wanted to come to be here but they are waiting for us because we will launch yet another mega-project as Governor Sahali calls them. These are the countries supporting the peace process, they greet you and I greet the beneficiaries of this development project — the people of Sibutu and the people of Sitangkai. (applause)

This project I very heartily agreed to to put in the State of the Nation Address not only because it was proposed by our very important ally and leader Congressman Jaafar but also because this project is in line with our Super Regions plan, the creation of five major regions including Agri-business Mindanao to spread development away from an inequitable concentration in Metro Manila.

Gaya nang napaliwanag ni Congressman Jaafar, ang proyektong ito ay mahusay na paraan upang palakihin ang kinikita ng mga taga-rito habang inaalagaan ang mga lamang-dagat na mamanahin ng inyong mga anak at apo. Sa ibang salita — sustainable development. Hindi lang mga mangingisda ang makikinabang dito, kung hindi pati na ang mga nag-aani ng perlas at agar-agar o seaweed.

Malaki ang inaasahang kikitain ng proyektong ito. Ang mga isdang aalagaan dito ay matataas ang halaga sa bilihin tulad ng mameng. Ang mameng ang endangered species (applause) pero kung aalagaan sa fish cage hindi pa rin pwede sabi ng aking anak na environmentalist. Pero yung lapu-lapu pwede pa, yung abalone pwede pa. Okay. So isang payo dahil gusto natin na alagaan ang kinabukasan — lapu-lapu, abalone, perlas, agar-agar. (applause) Kaya sa palaisdaan lamang, kung makakabuo kayo ng limang daang kilo tuwing anihan, hindi malayong umabot sa sandaang milyon piso ang kikitain ng komunidad. Hindi pa kasama dito yung perlas at agar-agar.

Pinakikinabangan ng proyektong ito ang likas na kaalaman ng mga taga-rito tungkol sa pag-aalaga at lamang-dagat. Tinanong ko nga kay Congressman Jaafar kanina bakit ang tawag mo modified mariculture. Sabi niya kasi hindi lamang yung scientific knowledge galing sa ibang lugar kung hindi yung likas na kaalaman ng taga-rito kasama sa inyong teknolohiya. Kinu-combine ito at ang mga siyentipikong pamamaraan nagmumula sa pananaliksik ng Mindanao State University Tawi-Tawi College of Technology and Oceanography. (applause)

Napag-usapan natin itong Tawi-Tawi College of Technology and Oceanography. May nakita ako yatang tseke dito na two million pesos galing sa CHED. Ito ay para sa scholarship program ng inyong eskwelahan dito kasi gusto natin… Ewan ko kung ilan ang mga naka-enrol sa fisheries dito. Ilan ba? Gusto natin doblehin, (applause) gusto natin maging 2,000 ang mag-aaral dito kaya meron kayong scholarship fund dito na two million pesos para makatulong na maging 2,000 ang inyong enrollment. (applause)

The way to peace begins with providing a person a job — ito magbibigay ng trabaho sa inyo. Providing a person food on the table — ito magbibigay ng pagkain sa inyo and, therefore, human dignity. Central to that success is to invest in the people — in education kagaya ng ating scholarship, in healthcare, roads, bridges na ilo-launch natin mamamaya sa Bongao.

Spending on physical and human infrastructure network remains central to the ability of Tawi-Tawi to break out of what Congressman Jaafar described — the poverty, the historic cycle of despair, and to prepare and to meet the challenges of the future. So we have and will continue to invest heavily in new spending. (applause)

Later, it will be in Bongao, right Jess? Jess Dureza will update us on the development programs in Mindanao. Baka yun na ang magiging huling report ni Secretary Jess Dureza dahil aalis na siya bilang Office of the Adviser on the Peace Process. Siguro ang susunod na pagbisita ko dito sa Tawi-Tawi pag kasama ko si Jess Dureza, pupunta na siya dito hindi bilang OPAP kung hindi bilang Press Secretary na ng ating bansa. (applause)

Anyway, Jess has done a great job, as OPAP and as BIMP-EAGA head where Tawi-Tawi is very important. Through him we have worked closely with out allies on an amazing number of public works projects. I talked about the bridges and roads.

Later today, we will launch the 324 million-peso Tawi-Tawi Bridge-Road Partnership Project, which is funded by the USAID, the DPWH and the ARMM government. It is a long road in the Tawi-Tawi mainland with a bridge to connect the five towns of the mainland to Bongao. This is important because we need infrastructure in Tawi-Tawi if we are going to bring Tawi-Tawi out of poverty.

And we are happy that our allies are only too willing to help Muslim Mindanao. When we were coming to Bongao in the airplane, the Ambassador of Saudi Arabia, Ambassador Wali gave me a note. Saudi Arabia has a loan with us of 20 million dollars which we are already implementing. But he also said that Saudi Arabia is willing to loan another 80 million dollars for priority projects in Muslim Mindanao. (applause) But we need not new ideas but those we already have, those that already have feasibility studies, those already in the pipeline, those already in the GAA budget. And so I have instructed, for one, Secretary Arthur Yap to put the projects in the pipeline, submit it for Saudi funding including the four phases of the Mariculture project of Tawi- Tawi. (applause)

And then because we want to double the enrollment of students in the schools of agriculture and fisheries in Muslim Mindanao, I asked Commissioner Ricafort and Commissioner Omar also to present a full-blown feasibility study and proposal to the Saudi fund for doubling the enrollment in the schools of the Autonomous Region for agriculture and fisheries. (applause)

Investing in vital infrastructure also includes building up our environment as a reflection of our commitment to our quality of life. So, with regard to the road that’s being built now or going to be built now in Bongao and in mainland Tawi-Tawi — although it will be implemented by the private sector — I count on Governor Sahali and the mayors of the towns directly benefiting from the Tawi-Tawi Bridge-Road Partnership Project to ensure that construction will be done with the least damage to the ecosystems of the project area.

Speaking of the environment, I am glad also that Congressman Jaafar mentioned the strategic location of Tawi-Tawi in the coral triangle. This is the largest almost contiguous collection of corals in the whole world. And the reason why I was asking my daughter Luli for her opinion on the mameng is that in the World Wildlife Fund she happens to be the policy officer of the coral triangle. (applause) So, please follow her advice on how to make mariculture here sustainable. In return, I will ask her to help you to also prepare project proposals for sustainable fisheries in the Autonomous Region of Muslim Mindanao that we can submit to funding for the Saudi fund. (applause)

Later today also in Bongao, we will visit a Madrasah being spruced up for the opening of classes because before the students can enter the Tawi- Tawi College of Technology and Oceanography they must first be able to finish grade school and high school. So, we will inspect the desks and the chairs which are part of the one billion-peso national budget for school furniture to prevent ‘standing room only’ or SRO schools.

ARMM being autonomous has his own DepEd. It has his own budget for education. But because we have provinces in the Autonomous Region that are very poor we are also providing help from national government budget including helping repair the Madrasahs and helping make sure that your school furniture is complete.

These investments in people and in physical infrastructure will in turn attract more of the private investment that Governor Jaafar says he is hoping for and it will create more jobs, ultimately improving the lives of the people of Tawi-Tawi. Congressman Jaafar has already mentioned the 2006 figure says Tawi-Tawi is the poorest province in the country. It has a poverty incidence of 7.89 percent, and we must do something together to bring that percentage down.

We are aware that no matter how much improvement there is at the top of the economic ladder, many of the people in Tawi-Tawi still struggle mightily. Dahil dito, ilulunsad rin natin mamaya yung programang Conditional Cash Transfer. Sa Bongao din yata yun gagawin. Tatlong libong pinakamahihirap na pamilya ng Tawi-Tawi ay makakatanggap ng pera buwan-buwan. Pero may mga kundisyon. Halimbawa, kailangan pumasok sa eskwela yung mga anak na sa edad pang-elementarya. Yan ang isang halimbawa, kailangan pumasok, ito ay buwan-buwan pero may kundisyon, kailangan pumasok sa eskwela ang mga anak na nasa edad elementarya.

At para lalo pang himukin ang mga magulang na mag-aral ang kanilang mga anak, at upang mabawasan ang gutom sa Tawi-Tawi, itinatag natin ang Food-for-School program. Sa programang ito — ewan ko kung nandito na yun, sana nandito na yun — yung mga estudyanteng elementarya ay mag-uuwi ng isang kilong bigas araw-araw (applause) para sa ganun — hindi pa ba ginagawa yun dito? O, ginagawa na — para sa ganun maiwasan ang gutom at may incentive para pumasok ang mga bata.

Mamaya pupunta tayo sa NFA bodega. We will help segregate rice for Food-for-School in a National Food Authority bodega.

I like to call what we are doing today our focus on three areas. The three “Es” — the Economy like this mariculture project, the Environment where we have to make sure the mariculture and the road projects all conform to the requirements of the coral triangle, and Education which I have mentioned several times in connection with the Tawi-Tawi College of Technology and Oceanography, the Food-for-School and the Madrasahs and the desks and chairs. These are central to lifting up the people of Tawi-Tawi.

Kanina binanggit ni Governor Sahali na apat na beses na raw ako nagpunta dito sa Tawi-Tawi. Yung apat na beses yun ang yung kasama siya. Pero sa katotohanan siguro mga anim na beses na ako nagpunta rito.

Kanina binanggit ni Congressman Jaafar na noong 1949 ba yun? 48, yung aking tatay ay nandito. Noong panahong yun siya ay Assistant Secretary ng Department of Foreign Affairs. Siya ang nag-negotiate para mabalik ang Turtle Island sa Pilipinas mula sa Malaya. (applause) Nung siya ay Vice-President na, 1958 yata, ay nagpunta siya muli sa Tawi- Tawi, nagpunta siya muli sa Turtle Islands at dala-dala niya yung kanyang anak, ako na nga yun. Kaya (applause) naalala ko doon sa bisitang yun ay binigyan ako ng regalo, isang malaking turtle. Nakatali parang yung mga kabayo o mga carabao na may tali, binigay sa akin. Tapos nakita ko yung turtle lumuluha. Umiiyak pala ang mga turtle. Sabi ko sa Nanay ko, “Ma, the turtle is crying.” Sabi nung Nanay ko… kasi noon dala ko… ang Tatay ko dala ang Nanay ko at saka dala ako. Ngayon dala ko ang asawa ko at saka dala ko rin ang anak ko. Kaya sabi ni Nur, history repeats itself. (applause) Sabi ko, “Ma, the turtle is crying.” So, yung Nanay ko pumunta doon sa… ewan ko kung Governor ng Tawi Tawi o — Sulu pa noon, di ba? Sulu pa — o Mayor ng Turtle Island, sabi niya, pakawalaan na lang natin yung turtle. So, pinakawala yung turtle, nanduon kami sa beach. Aba nung pinakawala yung turtle, ang bilis tumakbo hanggang dagat. Akala ko ang turtle symbol ng mahinang kumilos, marunong pala… ang turtle pala hindi lamang marunong umiyak, marunong din palang tumakbo. Pero mula noon talagang nakita natin dapat inaalagaan natin ang ating kalikasan. Kaya yung Turtle Islands bagamat hindi ako magbibisita doon nitong araw na ito, alagaan natin ng husto. Ito rin ay bahagi nung tinatawag na Turtle Islands Heritage Park, isa ring project ng World Wildlife Fund.

Yung naalala kong pangalawang bisita ko dito ay doon sa Balimbing, noong bagong halalan pa lamang at nung bagong halal pa lamang ako bilang Senador. At nagse-celebrate pa yung mga local officials dahil sila rin ay bagong halal. Di nagpunta ako doon, at sabi nila, “Eto, eto kain kayo dito meron tayong — ano’ng tawag dito? — yung roast calf.” Ay, ang daming roast calf naman sa Manila. So, “hindi,” sabi ko, tinitignan ko yung lobster. Naku! pina-uwi ako ng isang basket ng lobster. Yung yamang-dagat talaga ng Tawi-Tawi, yan ang ating aanihin ngayon sa inyong mariculture para sa ganun gaganda ang buhay ng ating taumbayan.

Naalala ko rin noong ako ay Vice-President, or Senador pa nga yata, Senador pa nga yata, isa pa. Nagpunta naman ako doon sa Mapun sa Cagayan de Tawi-Tawi. Kaya ako ay nasisiyahan na aside… tapos siyempre Bongao ilang beses na gaya ng sabi ni Governor Sahali at noong Vice-President ako doon sa Sitangkai — yung kapatid o tatay? — kapatid ni Mayor Ahaja ang mayor, yung Vice-Mayor ngayon, at ngayon — ah, noon bahagi pa ang Sibutu ng Sitangkai, ngayon hiwalay na — at nasisiyahan ako ngayon na narito tayo sa Sibutu. Kaya anim na bayan na sa labing ilang bayan ninyo ang nabisita ko. Pero naririnig ko na yung susunod na phases, mga binanggit na pangalan ni Congressman Jaafar ay mga bayang hindi ko pa napupuntahan. Kaya susunod na phase ng mariculture, imbitahin niyo muli ako para mabisita ko yung inyong limang bayan ng Tawi-Tawi. (applause)

To all of you, Congratulations! Ngayon ang simula ng pagbangon ng Tawi-Tawi.

Maraming salamat sa inyong lahat. (applause)
