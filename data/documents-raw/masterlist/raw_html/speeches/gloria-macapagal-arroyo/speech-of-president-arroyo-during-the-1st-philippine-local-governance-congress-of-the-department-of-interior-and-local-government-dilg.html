Speech of President Arroyo during the 1st Philippine Local Governance Congress of the Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG)

October 10, 2001

Speech
of
Her Excellency Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo
President of the Philippines
During the 1st Philippine Local Governance Congress of the Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG)

[Delivered at the Grand Ballroom, Manila Midtown Hotel, Ermita, Manila, October 10, 2001]

Maraming salamat Kalihim Joey Lina.

Pangulo ng ULAP, si Governor Dolfo del Rosario; Chairman ng League of Provinces, si Governor Louie Villafuerte; Pangulo ng League of Vice Governors, Vice Governor Herrera; Presidente ng League of Municipalities, Mayor Guico; Presidente ng League of Cities, Mayor Tolentino; Your Excellencies of the Diplomatic Corps; James Marty Lim, Presidente ng Liga ng mga Barangay; mga local executives; participants of the First Philippine Local Governance Congress; other guests; ladies and gentlemen; mahal kong mga kababayan:

Happy 10-10-10.

This is 10-10-10: October 10th 2001, the 10th anniversary of the local government code and on this day we begin by thanking and honoring the leaders who championed the passage of this the local government code 10 years ago — we start with President Cory Aquino who signed the bill into law 10 years ago today. We also thank Senate President Jovy Salonga and the late Speaker Ramon Mitra who exercised political leadership in shepherding the code through their respective chambers. We thank Senator Nene Pimentel and then Congressman Larry de Pedro who sponsored the bill in their respective chambers. And we acknowledge the Chairman of the League of Governors, Louie Villafuerte, who was a prime mover in forming the league of leagues which actively lobbied for the passage of this code.

These visionary leaders and their successors crafted a law that peacefully transformed our political system from a centralized government to a decentralized one.

Unlike many other nations like America, China, Germany, Japan, and Malaysia, where local governments existed before they banded together to create a central government, the Philippines’ history is one of central colonial government antedating local governments. The Local Government Code of 1991, therefore, was a move against the tide and it started us towards devolution. Today, 10 years later, the local governments face unprecedented fiscal, political, economic and technological challenges even after all the gains that Joey Lina spelled out because of the Local Government Code dahil alam ko na kahit sa ilalim ng nakaraang sampung taon ang dami nang ginansiyang ginawa ng local governments sa ilalim ng local government codes marami pa ring naiiwan ng hamon.

Kaya naman, sa unang araw ng aking pagkaupo bilang Presidente, pinili ko para maging alter ego ko sa DILG ang isa sa inyo, ang dati niyong Pangulo ng ULAP, si Secretary Joey Lina. Alam ko na si Joey, bilang isa sa mga sumulat ng Local Government Code sa Senado ay nanguna sa inyo noon upang siguraduhin na maprotektahan ang local autonomy at matanggap ninyo ang inyong IRA shares. Hanggang ngayon nga eh kinukulit pa rin ni Joey si Emy Boncodin at ako tungkol sa IRA ninyo.

At noong una kong weekend bilang Pangulo, andami kong naabutang pending na permiso ng mga Barangay Captain para makabiyahe. Sabi ko, “ano ako pa ang nagbibigay ng permiso para magbiyahe ang Barangay Captains?” Kaya nagdesisyon ako na mula noon idi-delegate ko ang permiso ng mga local government executives para magbiyahe sa Secretary ng Interior at Local Government.

At sang-ayon sa payo ni Joey, isa sa unang ginawa ko nung ako’y naging Presidente ay pinawalang bisa yung tatlong executive order ng dating administrasyon na nagbabawas ng inyong kapangyarihan. I am referring to: the E.O. of the previous administration which required you first to submit your annual investment plan to DBM before you could get your 20 percent development fund. Upon the advise of Joey I also repealed the E.O. which authorized DBM to withhold part of your IRA intended for your contributions to the GSIS, Philhealth and other government financial institutions. And Joey also recommended to me that I should abolish the E.O. which required you to set aside five percent of your 20 percent development fund for anti-drug abuse programs — not that we are against anti-drug abuse programs, but we trust you, the local governments, to make your decisions by yourselves.

The law of course requires that you still submit your annual investment plans to DILG and DBM, but this time it is for monitoring and oversight purposes and not as a prerequisite for the release of your money.

Kamakailan, ako ay nakipag-ugnayan sa mga pinuno ninyo sa ULAP sa pamumuno ni Governor Dolfo del Rosario. Pinag-aralan nga namin tungkol sa IRA at alam ko na ito ang hinihintay ninyo sa akin. Alam niyo marami tayo magkakilala ng matagal eh, alam niyo naman ang aking style — diretso sa punto, wala masyadong motherhood statements — kaya ito ang aming mga kasunduan:

Una, beginning the year 2002 — in other words the budget we submitted now for next year — the President’s budget that we submitted to Congress automatically appropriates the IRA. Kung ginagawa yon sa debt service, ba’t hindi sa debts to local governments?

Pangalawa, we abolished the LGSEF in the proposed 2002 Budget. Alam niyo yung LGSEF tinanggal sa inyo, nilagay sa Malacañang, tapos kung gusto niyo ng project dahil kulang yung inyong IRA tatakbo kayo sa Malacañang, luluhod magmamakaawa para humingi ng sarili ninyong pera. Kaya ngayon ‘pag may mga Mayors lumalapit sa akin para humingi ng projects sabi ko, “hindi na kayo kailangang pumunta rito, importante buo ang ira niyong natatanggap kasi yung LGSEF pera naman ninyo.”

Number three, last year in the previous administration and this year — because our 2001 budget is only the reenacted 2000 budget — your IRA allocation was P106 billion. Of the P106 billion allocated last year, the year 2000, the previous administration released P103 billion, so kulang ang ni-release. This year, 106 billion pa rin ang allocation dahil reenacted lamang, but even if the IRA budget for this year as allocated is only P106 billion, we are releasing P121 billion. What does this consist in? Yung P106 billion, yon lalabas yon, now on top of the 106 billion provided by the previous Congress for this year, the following payments of obligations of the previous administration but which my administration is complying with even if the debt is not my fault, we are paying you the following:

P4.05 billion representing the IRA withheld way back in 1999. We’ll be releasing this one billion pesos a month, and before this month ends, we will release the full and last of four tranches of this reserve.

Another release we are making is the P2.5 billion representing the unreleased balance of the LGSEF for last year, the year 2000.

And if you remember that last year you did not get your December IRA, so kahit na hindi ko kasalanan yon huhulugan namin yon, and that is why we will be releasing P2.25 billion representing one-forth of the IRA for December 2000 because we know that you need money for Christmas bonuses.

And we are releasing five billion pesos representing the LGSEF for this year 2001. I have already instructed Secretary Romulo, the Chair of the Oversight Committee on Devolution, to convene the committee to effect the release of your LGSEF for the year 2001, and as much as possible, we try to base this on the codal formula as recommended by ULAP.

With regard to the unreleased balance of the regular IRA due last year amounting to P6.75 billion, uulitin ko kahit na hindi ko utang ito, utang ng predecessor ko, we stand foursquare and acknowledge this commitment as part of our national obligation. Dahil may malaki akong budget deficit na minana sa predecessor ko, your ULAP leaders and I worked out a scheme to address this debt and at the same time address the financial situation in which we are right now.

We will treat this P6.75 billion as a contingent liability to the LGUs, in other words a guaranteed liability.

I have tasked the DBM, DILG, DOF, and the Executive Secretary, together with ULAP, to securitize this unreleased IRA as bonds for LGUs to enable you to use your uncollected IRA shares now and not wait for when the national government can afford to pay them. The bonds shall be floated by a government financial institution to fund your activities to the extent of your uncollected IRA. These bonds will be guaranteed by the national government.

And if the private banks, the commercial banks, buy these LGU bonds, they will be considered as complying with the Agri-agra law. That way they’ll be encouraged to buy your bonds. In turn, I ask that you spend the proceeds of these LGU bonds on activities related to agriculture or agrarian reform para naman talagang sang-ayon sa Agri-Agra Law. And if you do this spend them for agriculture or agrarian reform, including the salaries of your MOUs, and your MAROs and the MOOE — so may things that you can justify as related to agriculture and agrarian reform — this will form part of your answer to the call of the times, as I enunciated in my State of the Nation Address last July.

If you remember in my SONA, I stated my vision and mission: to be bold in our national ambitions so that our country will win the war against poverty within the decade.

And to succeed, our national agenda must revolve around four components — apat na elemento ng pakikibaka sa kahirapan.

The first is an economic philosophy of free enterprise appropriate to the 21st century.

Pagnenegosyo upang dumami ang trabaho.

And the local governments can do so much to promote free enterprise and job creation, and therefore I ask our local governments — as Joey said you are already doing — to streamline your operations and slash red tape in order to promote investments under a free enterprise system. There must be continuity between national and local governments in their efforts to be investor-friendly.

Sabi ng mga negosyante sa ibang bansa ‘pag ako’y nakikipag-usap sa kanila ay noon pa ang daming repormang ginawa sa BOI, sa mga national agencies, kaya sanay sila na isa na lang account officer ang kausap nila. Kaya lang pag-abot sa local governments iba na naman ang kausap. Yon ang sinasabi nating seamless efforts. ‘Yan ang sana ang BOI at ang local governments ang magkaroon ng isang unit lamang para isa na lamang ang kausap ng ating mga investors.

The second part of our agenda is a modernized agricultural sector founded on social equity. Palalaguin ang kita at ani ng maralitang taga-bukid, at dito sana gastusin niyo ng husto yung proceeds ng LGU bonds na ibibigay, papagamit sa inyo ng mga bangko ngayon kaysa sa maghihintay pa hanggang kaya muli ng national government.

And the third component is a social bias toward the disadvantaged to balance our economic development plan. Pagkalinga sa mga bahagi ng lipunan na naiiwanan ng kaunlaran. At alam naman natin na sa local government code, yung mga ahensya na-devolve sa inyo yon na nga ang ahensyang mga social bias — yung health, yung social welfare, yung agriculture — kaya talagang nasa inyo na, kayo na ang frontliner dito sa social bias sa ating development plan.

And the fourth component is to raise the moral standards of government and society. Moralidad sa gobyerno at lipunan bilang saligan ng tunay na kaunlaran.

And so having talked about these four components of our national agenda, may I take this opportunity to enlist the local governments to stand in the front line of this war against poverty as we take strides in delivering better services to our communities.

During my State of the Nation Address, I called for a moratorium on politicking.

Today that moratorium is more urgent than ever.

The New York incident of September 11 has changed the global mood. World business conditions are more uncertain now. And so more than ever, we must now rely more on our own efforts and our own domestic economy. And more than ever, we must avoid lapsing into a sense of drift and losing momentum.

After the September 11 terrorist attacks, the Philippine government pledged to support the international coalition in the new fight against terrorism. And let me just take this opportunity to thank the local governments of Lanao del Sur, the Mayors League, because they visited me yesterday to tell me that they are foursquare behind the position I have taken on international terrorism.

This shows that the battle is not against Islam. The battle is against terrorism whatever the religion of the terrorists.

After the military action of the United Nations in Afghanistan, I reiterated our commitment.

And so, because we are now in this battle, we must strengthen our internal focus against terrorism. The Armed Forces of the Philippines and the Philippine National Police are going all out against the lawless violence engendered by the terrorist threats. But you, the local government units and institutions down to the barangay level, are the core of this effort.

And so I urge all of you to take an active part and even take the leadership, if you wish, in this battle against terrorism in your own local government areas I urge all LGUs to adopt the Patrol 117 approach initiated by the DILG for the quick response to crimes and violence. I likewise urge you to involve the participation of citizens in crime prevention.

The international situation arising from the terrorist attacks in America and the military action in Afghanistan asks all of us to reenergize and refocus ourselves towards achieving our nation’s priorities.

And because we have even added challenges because of these new events in the world today, we all realize that the IRA is inadequate to meet all the demands of devolution and all the demands of the new international situation. Thus, it is important that local governments must be bold in exercising your corporate powers to explore alternative financing options and increase your ability to raise local revenues. In this regard, I am happy to see the active participation of private financial institutions and the donor community in this gathering. They are celebrating with us and they are celebrating the role they have played in promoting devolution over this last 10 years

We and they are celebrating today the adolescence though not yet the full maturity of local government autonomy — well 10 years old is really just entering adolescence after all. As we celebrate a decade of devolution, let us buckle down to work once again and collectively address the challenges in our continuing journey for a meaningful autonomy so that this adolescence will turn into a truly mature autonomy in the next years to come.

Congratulations and Happy Decade Anniversary.
