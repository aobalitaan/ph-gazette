Speech of the Honorable Ferdinand E. Marcos, President of the Philippines, on Farmers' Day

I am very happy to be with the true heroes of our country, the farmers, on this occasion. Farmers are more deserving of praise than those we recognize as brave men of our nation. Although they did not participate in the field of war or politics, every day, the farmer pours out sweat and blood… Every day, they struggle against the trials of nature… Every day, they risk their entire being so that our country does not suffer from hunger.

Agriculture is the cornerstone of Filipino livelihood. The lives and fortunes of our nation depend on the sweat of millions of farmers, a large part of our citizenry. Some so-called experts say that our country is backward or lagging in development because our livelihood depends on agriculture. This argument of these so-called experts is based on the mistaken belief that the measure of progress is large factories, steel plants and other signs of industrialization. I say this is a mistaken belief because events in the present time have proven that it is not enough for a country to have tons of steel to ensure the progress and prosperity of the people of a nation. In the present time when almost every country is experiencing an economic crisis, it is clear that countries with sufficient food for their people are surviving. The serious problem of every country in the world today only proves that first of all, sufficient food and the needs of the people are necessary. We Filipinos are fortunate because the Creator has given us a land rich in natural resources. Our fields, our seas, our forests possess resources that are hard to match throughout the world. Although we have a problem due to the energy crisis, this crisis has shown that many of the developed countries, the countries that rely on industry, like the ancient civilizations buried in the forgotten pages of history, also have feet of clay. It is easy to prove the instability of some of the so-called developed or industrialized countries: we just need to ask them if that oil can quench thirst? Can that steel or iron be served as a dish? Can those chemicals or plastics be made into food if not exchanged for food? And a time will come, which seems to be gradually approaching, when the true value of agriculture in the world's livelihood will be recognized again.

This is why the policy of our government is focused on high food production… Land reform… Development of our villages. Indeed, we see a future that indicates that the life and survival of our country rests solely on the ability of our villages to provide food for our entire country, and on the harvesting of the ingredients needed to make the basic needs of our people. The history of our country's development for many centuries has been focused on the villages. The first cry of our desire for independence was more deeply felt by millions of Filipino farmers who united to break the chains of our slavery. Many of our soldiers and defenders of independence who remained with the spirit of freedom during the dark days of the past war were young farmers with native courage and love of freedom and democracy. Our own movement for National Reform, our New Society, was born out of our sincere pledge to eliminate and correct the injustices that have prevailed in our villages for many years. We, as a race, are proud of the monuments of service and change that have taken place in our fields. After many years as a country that still buys rice, the Philippines is now selling rice to other countries, we have increased the income of farmers. With the help of the various programs we have launched in the fields, we can overcome even the worst problems in this time of crisis. We just need to look back at the latest events in the villages to discover the resilience and self-confidence we need to face the challenges and problems of the coming years.

Our land reform has freed millions of farmers from their bondage to the land. As of March 1981, we have distributed 495,904 certificates of land transfer covering 589,649 hectares of farmland to 378,789 farmers. We know that the lack of sufficient facilities leaves the villages behind in development. That is why we launched a program to lend money to farmers, including Masagana 99, Masaganang Maisan, Biyaya ng Dagat, Bakahan sa Baranggay and Gulayang Bayan. In Masagana 99 alone, we were able to lend P4.5 billion as of 1980. Despite what our opponents say, 80 percent of the farmers who borrowed money paid their loans. The number of usurers charging exorbitant interest rates in the farmlands decreased from 90 percent in 1961 to 35 percent in 1976. Due to the success of Masagana 99, lending money was also extended to corn, vegetable gardens, cattle raising, fishermen, and even forest products.

To ensure our bountiful harvests at all times, the government subsidizes the cost of fertilizer. As of December 1930, the government has provided two billion pesos in aid for fertilizer used in the rice and corn fields of farmers. In our desire to ensure a good price for the farmers' harvest, the government created the National Grains Authority in 1972, which was tasked with buying and selling grains, especially rice. In 1975, we only had 52 rice mills for 3.2 million cavans of rice. Currently, we have 792 rice mills in various parts of the country that can accommodate 36.2 million cavans. As part of this program, we have set the price of rice to ensure that farmers benefit from their hard work in case the price of rice falls during the harvest season.

The government continues to provide irrigation in various parts of the country without letup. In 1972, only 730,158 hectares throughout the Philippines had irrigation. As of 1980, 1,322,390 hectares had irrigation, or a total of 70 percent of the number of hectares that are now irrigated. In the next ten years, the government will provide irrigation for 2.47 million hectares of land, which is 73 percent of the land that can be irrigated in our country.

Our government has allocated P500 million for electricity in the countryside, in addition to P282,352,000 from USAID, the World Bank, OECF and the Republic of Germany to bring the true signal of development to our villages. As of today, 1,441,000 homes in the countryside now have electricity.

Just recently, I signed PD 1467 which launched the National Crop Insurance Program and created the Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation, which will insure the crops of approximately 1.6 million farmers in case of any calamity caused by nature, or if crops become sick due to plant pests. This crop insurance is the first of its kind and covers all types of farm produce, including expenses for planting, fertilizers, medicines, the farmer's share of the insurance premium, and other necessities with the assistance of the supervised loan program.

Our efforts to improve yields are not limited to what I have mentioned. Along with all of this are our projects related to research, exchange of experiences and knowledge, establishment of cooperatives, and promotion of product exports to other countries. We are doing all of this because more than anyone else, we truly know the shortcomings of our livelihood based on agriculture. In order to meet the food needs of our growing population, to increase the income of those in the countryside so that our progress will be faster, to completely eliminate the gap we have long known between the countryside and urban areas, we must continue our efforts to develop the countryside and develop agriculture.

In light of these developments, I have decided to announce today some policies and measures of the government to ensure the progress of the livelihood of our farmers.

For rice and corn farmers experiencing difficulties with the rising cost of goods, I authorize the setting of the price of rice at P1.55 per kilo and corn at P1.30 per kilo. I hope that this will encourage farmers to be more diligent in harvesting abundant crops. To further help our farmers, starting today, the government will launch a rice drying program with an initial fund of P106 million.

One of the main objectives of our food program is to ensure that our countrymen have sufficient food available for purchase at all times. However, there is one area of food production that we are neglecting, and that is regarding milk and dairy products. To rectify this situation, and also to save the dollars we spend on importing these products from other countries, today, we will launch the dairy development program by appointing three representatives from the private sector who will form the board of directors of the Dairy Development Corporation.

In the past few years, we have successfully transformed our village organizations into cooperatives. Currently, we have 21,000 village organizations throughout the country, composed of millions of farmers. To further strengthen these farmers' cooperatives, I am ordering the release of the reserved funds of the barangay, in accordance with the rules of the Central Bank. I am also authorizing the Land Bank to deposit the corresponding funds in the Cooperative Rural Banks. At least one million pesos per cooperative bank will be deposited. And I am also ordering, starting today, that the Cooperative Development Loan Fund be made a revolving fund so that farmers can benefit from and borrow from it.

To further assist our cooperatives, I authorize the Ministry of Agriculture to use the two million pesos it has saved for the training of field personnel who will establish the cooperatives. In addition, I empower the National Economic Development Authority to allocate an additional six million pesos to the Cooperative Foundation of the Philippines as initial capital. And so that our countrymen who were led astray and are now returning to the fold of the law and laying down their arms after pledging allegiance to our flag can also enjoy the benefits of cooperatives in the countryside, I also ordered the reopening of the Amanah Bank to provide the funds needed by our countrymen who fought against the government but are now returning to our ranks and uniting with our principles.

And to ensure the protection of their welfare and rights, starting today, I direct that our farmers have representation in offices related to agriculture, such as the Livestock Development Council, Dairy Development Corporation, and National Wages Council.

I expect that you were not too shocked or overwhelmed by the successive policies and measures that I have announced for the sake of our farmers. The farmer is closest to the heart of your government. Your servant strives to provide you with all the blessings, help, and support needed to advance agriculture in our country. You can rely on your servant not to stop until we have fully achieved the dreamed progress for the farmer. Your servant will not relent until the condition of the farmer is placed at its peak, because in your hands and on your shoulders, in your strength, truly lies the life, fate, and future of our nation.
