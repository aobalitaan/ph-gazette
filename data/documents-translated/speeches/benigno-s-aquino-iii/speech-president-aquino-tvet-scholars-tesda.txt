Speech of the Honorable Benigno S. Aquino III, President of the Philippines, in tribute to TESDA scholars.

From the very start of our term, it was clear to us: We are not content with the concept of a “trickle-down” effect. This was the belief in the past, where focus was only on economic growth, and waiting for its benefits to flow down to the fringes of society. Because here, we are just relying on luck; we also know how long it takes for its positive effects to reach our countrymen. If our countrymen also lack sufficient skills to contribute to progress, economic growth is meaningless to them.

That is why our vow is: inclusive growth. On the Straight Path, no one will be left behind. We thought of and launched various programs to empower our citizens to participate in the progress of society. Today, together with TESDA, one of the agencies promoting what we call inclusive growth, we celebrate the success of the Technical Vocational Education and Training (TVET) program. Likewise, we pay tribute to the transformation in the lives of our scholars. To all of you, congratulations. [Applause]

When we took office, we identified one of the reasons why many of our countrymen are unemployed and left behind. This is the jobs-skills mismatch, or the lack of alignment between the skills of graduates and the needs of our industries. Consequently, many end up in low-quality jobs with meager salaries. In this situation, how can one care for a family and secure their future? If we had allowed this problem to persist, we would have allowed the cycle of suffering for many of our countrymen to lengthen and worsen.

So we immediately spoke with the industries; we asked what their needs were. They responded with cooperation, even accepting trainees in their companies themselves. Now, there are 8.4 million course graduates of TVET. [Applause] Let me clarify, we are referring to courses here; and the graduates, there are some graduates who take more than one course, so that is not 8.4 million people.

The Training for Work Scholarship Program or TWSP is just one part of TVET. With the help of the private sector, we are training our scholars in various fields such as IT-BPM, semiconductor and electronics, agri-fishery, tourism, general infrastructure, and automotive. This August, the number of graduates here was 877,943. What's even better is that, according to a study, 71.9 percent of them immediately found employment [applause]—this is far from the 28.5 percent employment rate before. If we look at some industries, the employment rate of graduates of TESDA and Semiconductor and Electronics Industries in the Philippines or SEIPI reaches 91.26 percent—just a little more, 100 percent, or every graduate here will find a job. [Applause] I would like to guarantee that. But, if the graduate doesn't look for a job, they might not get one.

Among the beneficiaries of TWSP are Merryll, Renji, and Cristina. We heard their stories after training at TESDA. This is just proof that what we promised, we are fulfilling not just in words, but more importantly, in deeds. Through training that lasts for a few months, we can immediately provide a decent and dignified livelihood for so many Filipinos. That is the principle of the Straight Path: benefits are provided not tomorrow, but now. And if possible, as Linda Baldoz said, yesterday would have been better. [Laughter] When a program is this good, and we see positive results, it should be strengthened. The fund for TESDA in 2010 was P2.8 billion; by 2015, we almost doubled that to P5.3 billion. [Applause] Thank you to our colleagues in the Legislature.

Aside from TWSP, there are of course our enthusiastic graduates of the Sari-sari Store Training and Access to Resources or STAR Program of TESDA and Coca-Cola. [Applause] In this program, we provide training for women on professional business management. Imagine, it teaches bookkeeping, inventory management, accounting, and other skills to grow their income. For example, instead of selling raw ingredients, they sell cooked food, which adds to their income. Even how to ensure that the additional income goes to the right place is taught in the STAR program. This June, it reached 35,000 graduates, and the target of Coca-Cola and the government is to reach 200,000 graduates before 2020. Thank you to our partner, Coke. [Applause]

I often recall what Sec Joel told me. As he said, there was a sari-sari store owner who used to earn P800 a day; now, P4,000 a day. This is five times his income thanks to the STAR program. [Applause] It reminds me that our salaries are now the same, but he has no complaints, no stomach aches, no thinning hair. [Laughter] He has no “other benefits.”

Another meaningful program of TESDA is the Special Training for Employment Program or STEP. It is free training conducted in barangays regarding entrepreneurship and community service. Beneficiaries are provided with starter toolkits to serve as capital for their livelihood. Since its implementation began in July 2014, the number of beneficiaries of the program: 43,800 graduates. [Applause]

I once shared the story of one of the STEP graduates, Ms. Jennifer Noble of Batangas City. Before she trained at TESDA, she was only selling cold drinks and had no permanent job. Back then, she was lucky if she earned P300 a day. When TESDA gave her free training, and after completing the Beauty Care and Wellness Massage course, after a year, Jennifer had two salons. Now, she earns at least P2,000 a day. [Applause]

They, along with scholars like you, are just part of the thousands of our countrymen who have been and are being helped by TESDA, and are now helping their families and communities. These programs are truly worthwhile and meaningful. Because of the success you graduates have achieved, your government is able to continue them. The taxes you contribute to the national treasury also help provide opportunities for many more of our countrymen.

It's really different when the leader is like Secretary Joel Villanueva. [Applause] His predecessor, despite having already accomplished so much as you all know, even has the audacity to file a lawsuit now. Secretary Joel is a loyal, hardworking, and dependable public servant. Even when he was still a student, Joel was already a well-known athlete. Wherever he played on a team, Joel was considered a star player. Of course, you can only become a good athlete or player if you pour in hard work and dedication; if you have discipline and are willing to sacrifice in training. Joel truly worked hard for what he achieved. That's why we are confident that whatever he supports and teaches, he isn't just pretending to know. He isn't content with just reading books; learning theories and concepts of a field isn't enough for him; he actually does and tries them out. Now, even in the field of being a barista, Joel is a certified barista. [Applause] He uses his talent and experience to further improve the programs offered by the agency.

Let me repeat: Before, perhaps, the TESDA administrator would just be happy if we had graduates, that was enough. Secretary Joel, in collaboration with our DOLE, our DepEd, and other government agencies—first, the studies have meaning, and second, they ensure a future for the graduates. [Applause] I remember Joel reminding me of a movie I watched called “Thunderstruck,” starring NBA player Kevin Durant. What he said stuck with me: “Hard work beats talent when talent fails to work hard.”

This Joel truly doesn't lack in skill, hard work, and enthusiasm, which is why he reached his potential. And it's clear: He shows the same qualities in his public service; he maximizes every opportunity to help Filipinos improve their lives. I'm not surprised that many believe in his abilities, because frankly, he has already proven himself and will continue to do so. [Applause] I have full confidence: Joel is the leader the nation needs, [applause] who we are sure will help more people and improve more livelihoods once he is elevated to a higher position. [Applause] It seems your applause is a bit weak. You probably don't agree. [Applause]

It's clear: TESDA is proof of the positive transformation of our institutions. Your agency is a model of how the Straight Path addresses the needs of our Bosses, and in having a government focused on delivering long-term benefits, especially to those who need it most.

We have already attended many gatherings of TESDA, which is concrete proof of the increasing opportunities in our country. If activities like these will fill our schedule, I think, even if we celebrate every day, that’s okay. [Applause] As I said, we are used to, when Secretary Joel Villanueva and TESDA invite us, there are no questions, we go without a second thought. [Applause]

Truly: On the Straight and Narrow Path, we exhaust all our efforts to improve the situation of our Bosses. We continue to invest and pour support into education, health, and our social services. And because of our contribution, we are now reaping the fruits of our collective effort.

As I said during the SONA: This is just the beginning of all the achievements we have attained. According to experts, if the Straight and Narrow Path is not derailed, within a generation, we will supposedly be a first-world country. If good governance that is the source of economic growth continues, it won't be long before we equal, if not surpass, the developed countries. [Applause]

In the remaining time of my term, we will continue to do what is right and reasonable to lay down more opportunities in our country. But as my father said before, “I can open the door for you; but it is up to you, to keep it open.” It is truly up to you if you will step inside; in the end of all our efforts, the maximizing of opportunities is in your hands, progress is in your hands.

In 2016, the nation will once again face a crossroads, where we will choose our next leaders. The question is: Will all that we have worked for, all that we have achieved, simply vanish because of one election? May we unite once more and walk in the same direction; let us continue the reforms that will make the changes we are enjoying permanent; let us continue the leadership that has been done and will continue to do what is right for the Filipino people; let us continue to walk the Straight Path—the path where no one is left behind in progress. [Applause]

In closing, to you graduates and those currently taking courses, I earnestly appeal. You will be given opportunities, but your success is in your hands. As they say in English, every success has its story: Failure is an orphan, success has many fathers. When asked, “Will others be given sufficient opportunities?” The answer is, “Yes.” Especially if you show that the resources and time invested in you were truly worthwhile, and that you have borne fruit. That is my earnest appeal. Let us improve and take advantage of the opportunities you have received so that you can pass them on to those who will follow in your footsteps.

And finally, I saw Gladys, and it reminded me of Joel. What happens when someone has a path they believe they should take? In 2013, Joel was thinking of running for senator. I told Joel, “I don’t think so, brother, let’s skip 2013. Maximize what you can do at TESDA.” And I believe that when he agreed, many benefited because we had Joel Villanueva steering TechVoc. [Applause] In five years, he changed the face of TechVoc. From being a place that seemed hopeless, it is now the path to earning a higher salary than the President of the Republic. [Laughter and applause]

I remember when Joel was courting Gladys, it seemed Joel knew that Gladys was the one for him. [Laughter] Joel went through a long trial, if my memory of the story is correct. That’s why I look at Joel’s life and say, “Joel went through a long trial. Perhaps my trial is also nearing its end.” [Laughter and applause] And Joel gives us only one source of inspiration.

So my colleagues, let me reiterate, the future – it’s not true that there is already a predetermined fate for us. We create our own destiny, now, with the help of God Almighty, He will give us the path to achieve all our dreams.

Good afternoon. Thank you all very much.
