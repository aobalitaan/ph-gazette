Speech of the Honorable Benigno S. Aquino III, President of the Philippines, During lunch with the Ateneo de Manila Blue Spikers and Lady Eagles and De La Salle University Lady Spikers

Good afternoon to all of you again.

At least, this luncheon of ours pushed through, and I’d like to thank both Father Jett and Brother Armin for organizing this, and I really felt this was a very timely event. So, this is the formal portion of the speech… You know, I could see in your faces there are a lot of questions in your mind. I think first and foremost are two questions: One, when will we eat , and, is there enough for everybody?

Susan, I’m glad you’re here. I hope you took note that these are athletes who normally have healthy appetites.

You know, the rivalry between Ateneo and La Salle has really lasted for decades. I remember back in my time, which was about five years ago or so, [laughter] the competition was intense. I think if evil glares could literally kill, many generations of Ateneans and Lasallians would have already been wiped out.

Until now, the competition between these two schools is still heated. Whether it's basketball, volleyball, or other competitions, tickets are always in demand—I think for Fr. Jett, after those appealing for admission to Ateneo [laughter] getting a ticket is his second biggest problem—[Laughter] just to watch the game. It doesn't choose an age; whatever year you graduated, everyone, all-out in cheering for their respective alma mater. How can it be otherwise, because whenever they face each other, it always feels like life is at stake. Whether it's a championship game or a game with no bearing, it's the same; everyone will really give their all, just so they don't lose to the opponent.

Now, if you notice our Cabinet, there are those who studied in Katipunan, those who studied in Taft and Greenhills, and there are those who are unsure where they want to go [laughter] like Kim Henares who came from La Salle and Ateneo, and then Jun Abaya—he went to Taft, then Ateneo Law School by way of Annapolis, that's the school, not the street, you see. [Laughter] Nevertheless, we consider each other family. On all occasions, we have unity, except for, and this is it—a singular time when the Cabinet is not united, when Ateneo and La Salle are competing. [Laughter]

For example, once, I was in a room here in Malacañang with Brother Armin and Secretary Babes Singson, both from La Salle. It was UAAP Season last year, and it was probably the finals of the Men’s Basketball Tournament or the round of four. Secretary Babes asked Brother Armin, “How did our team do?” Brother Armin replied, “Ateneo lost to NU.” [Laughter] I didn't know if that was directed at me. I said, “Isn't the question, ‘How did our team do?’ But you're talking about Ateneo and NU?” I said, “Is that how you're taught at La Salle, to not answer a question directly? And you're a Christian brother at that.” [Laughter] Then, we all laughed.

Once, there was a State Visit here in Malacañang. And every time we would troop down the line, the PSG Band would play “Blue Eagle, The King!” Sec. Babes smilingly said, “Why is it that in occasions like this, I always hear ‘Fly High’?” My response was, “Because if we played ‘When The Saints Go Marching In,’ our guests might start dancing instead of marching.” [Laughter]

It also happened that last year and this year, coinciding with the finals match of the Ateneo and La Salle women’s volleyball teams, I was in Baguio for the PMA graduation. I wanted to watch, but I couldn’t, because it also happened that we had an issue that needed to be focused on and studied. But truthfully, I didn’t need to watch on TV; just from the cheers of our colleagues who were watching it, I knew how intense the game was, and who was winning.

What pleased me was when I read the newspaper the following day of this last match. The picture I saw: Some were wearing green and some were wearing blue, embracing each other. The game was over, and you players were sincerely congratulating each other. Despite the fierce competition and intense pressure to win, you demonstrated the spirit of unity and sportsmanship. Isn’t that wonderful?

Now, someone suggested that we accommodate a courtesy call from the Women’s Volleyball Team of Ateneo de Manila University, like last year. I thought: Maybe we could also invite the 1st runner-up of the tournament. If the Lady Eagles showed off their skills, they were likely motivated to prepare thoroughly because the competition wasn’t easy. Isn’t it true that when you have a skilled opponent, you are really encouraged to give your all? That is why we accepted today and recognize the Women’s Volleyball Team of De La Salle University, the DLSU Lady Spikers. Likewise, we received news of the championship win of the Men’s Volleyball Team of Ateneo de Manila University, the Ateneo Blue Spikers. I said, it would be good if we combined and unified your visits here.

Therefore, I congratulate those who comprise these three teams, from your members inside and outside the court, the players, coach and manager, as well as the institutions you represent. Indeed: You, student-athletes, reflect the vibrancy, enthusiasm, and determination of the current generation. You are models of the extraordinary talent and capability of young Filipinos to succeed, in whatever field they choose or cherish.

This gathering further proves that: Behind the intense competition and banter, there are true teammates and friends. This is understood, especially by you athletes who were once schoolmates, now rivals in various leagues. Some of your players also know this, as my writers – more or less your age – say, have sweethearts or crushes from the opposing school. [Laughter]

In this time when there are those pushing for chaos and division in various parts of the world, and even here with us, much can be learned from the good example you show. Even though you are now at the center of this age-old rivalry between Ateneo and La Salle, you do not completely succumb to the conflict and teasing. At your age, you show, even to those older than you, the power of humility and camaraderie.

I also thought: I hope that politicians, as well as members of various sectors of society, would demonstrate this kind of relationship as well. When they look at a conflict or competition, I hope they ask themselves: Is this rivalry healthy? And when there is criticism, I hope they ask: Is it constructive?

That is the spirit we want to instill in the minds and hearts of our countrymen. Even if we are divided into different islands, even if we have differences, in the end, we are still one team of Filipinos. No spike is too difficult to receive if we are focused on a unified goal. No attack is too strong to overcome if we have strategy, passing, and coordination among each other. In unity and harmony, in contributing to genuine change, every Filipino wins.

That is the call to us now, especially to you, the youth who will inherit the reforms we are sowing: to fight One Big Fight against corruption and poverty. As Coach Tai of the Ateneo Lady Eagles said: Heartstrong. As long as we are united and have a heart for our fellowmen, we are sure to achieve our collective aspirations as a nation.

Before I end, I would like to thank all of you who attended today. If someone requests a selfie with me again, I will agree, but with one condition this year. Last year, someone approached me and asked if they could have a selfie with me. I said, “Yes.” The person who asked me was just one, but in the photo, there were about twenty-five of us. [Laughter] But the photographer was good, no one’s face was cropped out. Before I agree, if anyone still wants a selfie with me, please have a selfie with our two colleagues who made this gathering possible first: Fr. Jett Villarin and Bro. Armin Luistro, and if Bro. Benny is also here, I hope you treat them with the same respect you would treat me.

Good afternoon to all of you once again. And congratulations to all. We would not have achieved our achievements without the good interaction with others.
