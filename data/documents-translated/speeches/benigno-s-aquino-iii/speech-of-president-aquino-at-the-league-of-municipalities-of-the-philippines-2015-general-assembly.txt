Speech of the Honorable Benigno S. Aquino III, President of the Philippines, at the 2015 General Assembly of the League of Municipalities of the Philippines

My concentration was good when I came here. I thought I could answer all of your questions. I already saw George. My concentration was broken. [Laughter] I’ve been friends with him for a long time because we met in college back then. He was there for eight years before he finally graduated, I think. [Laughter] No, because he was rebellious back then. Still is, I think.

I get nervous when I see him; we always end up laughing. Sometimes it’s “laugh at” instead of “laugh with,” but it doesn’t matter – that’s not my intention today.

Comrades, as President, you know that we have many issues that need to be addressed; every region, every province, and every municipality like yours, has challenges and problems that need our attention. If I could, I would visit each of your municipalities personally to listen to you. But you know, we only have one body, and there are also many national and foreign issues that consume our time. Luckily, we have organizations like the LMP, so we have the opportunity to meet in one hall, and collectively lay out our agenda to improve the living standards of our Bosses. To the mayors who really traveled far to be with us today, thank you very much to all of you. [Applause]

I know, our local government units play a big role. If running the entire country is the responsibility of the national government, you in the LGUs are the ones who directly interact with our citizens. You are the bridge between the national agenda and every corner of our country. You are the source, and the destination of the projects we implement. Through you, our citizens feel whether the government is true to its promise to serve the country. That is why: The success or failure of an administration depends on the cooperation of local government units with the national government.

I don't think anyone would disagree: Your job as local leaders is really difficult. I know the problems you face, which you are really immersed in, 24/7, in the local context. Although I have not been a mayor, we have also mixed with local politics. Maybe at first, we were just smiling. But as the days passed, we really felt the weight of our responsibility. I already told you this before: When I was newly elected Congressman in Tarlac, a councilor approached me. He needed help renting an orchestra for our fiesta. I asked, “How much do you need?” He replied to me, “P40,000 each.” How many do we need? He said, “We need two, and that’s the cheapest.” He added, so that there would be a backup, he said they needed two orchestras. In total: P80,000. In my mind, even if I doubled my salary at that time of 35,000 gross, it still wouldn’t be enough for his request. Just for the music, our funds would be insufficient.

That is just one example in a very long list of requests made to us as leaders, from the smallest to the biggest. As others say, what’s worse is that when someone asks for ten and you give nine or find a way to fulfill nine, the only thing they will remember is the one you couldn’t give. Is that right? [Applause]

It is unavoidable that some of us, in wanting to fund these requests, are tempted to create schemes or magic, to ensure that whenever they encounter someone offering a favor, they can grant it.

The result: A system where our constituents are perpetually dependent on favors, while leaders are also bound to give them. This practice has reigned for so long that it is now difficult to think of other ways of doing politics. Others ask: Is there even an alternative path?

Our response: There is, and that is precisely the path we are taking on the straight and narrow. Don't we have a saying: Instead of giving a man a fish, teach him how to fish?

We are strengthening our national institutions and empowering our local governments. We are promoting a system that truly responds to the needs of the people; where aid is not a grace or a favor, not obtained by offering a favor to a patron or through connections, not dependent on the whims of those in power, but a right obtained by following the correct policies.

We also believe this: Those who excel in service deserve to be incentivized. We are able to do this through our Seal of Good Housekeeping, which has been implemented since 2011, and the Seal of Good Local Governance in 2014. Those who receive these awards get a Performance Challenge Fund, and their ability to address the problems in their areas of responsibility is increased. Because of this, it is not surprising that in the coming years, more and more will excel and further develop their communities. Just a reminder, as the English speakers say, “Success has many fathers and failure is an orphan.”

And if we are talking about good governance, we do not need to look far. There is the good example shown by your head in the League of Municipalities of the Philippines, Mayor Sandy Javier. [Applause] Under his leadership, his town quickly recovered from the devastation of Typhoon Yolanda. But instead of just relaxing and being content with what he has done, he led the clearing of debris from the roads, even in his neighboring towns. I also heard that: For 36 hours, despite being sleepless and hungry, he and his colleagues continued to work non-stop to help others who were more severely affected by Yolanda. [Applause]

Indeed: With our concern and trust in each other, we have already achieved great changes in our country. With good governance, the economy continues to grow, which in turn improves our ability to fund our social services.

In the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program: If it was only 800,000 before, now, 4.4 million households are beneficiaries. In PhilHealth, we have also expanded the services we deliver. In education, we have filled the inherited backlog in classrooms, textbooks and chairs to further improve the quality of education of our youth. We are truly improving the lives of many of our countrymen; expanding opportunities to move towards progress, and raising their ability to contribute to the development of society.

We are continuously implementing transformations in the infrastructure sector. Recently, we inaugurated the Lullutan Bridge in Isabela. “Lullutan” is an Ibanag word. When it was first mentioned to me, it sounded like “lulutang” (will float). I said, why would a bridge be named “will float”? Perhaps that bridge will float and sink. And truthfully, the bridge that was already there in 1995 was called a spillover bridge. That means, when the water is high, the bridge will be covered.

In 1995, this bridge was damaged, causing immense suffering to our countrymen in Isabela. Instead of rebuilding the bridge, those before us only made temporary repairs. The result: longer travel times and higher transportation costs because a barge had to be used for communities to cross. If you don't use a barge, your trip will take an additional one and a half to two hours because you have to go around through different towns. We can't help but wonder: why were our countrymen allowed to suffer for so long, and why were they denied the services they deserved? That was twenty years, almost a generation passed, when it could have been completed in three years.

Another example: the Jalaur River Multipurpose Project II in Iloilo, which was supposedly conceived in the 1960s. We both emerged in people’s minds in 1960. But it is only now, during our administration, that it is being concretized. In addition to this, there is also the Aluling Bridge in Ilocos Sur, whose plan was laid out as early as 1978, but we are the ones who finished it. If you think about it, if these had been built a long time ago, how much progress could have been achieved by so many of our countrymen?

These are just a few examples of the transformations happening now under our good governance. Wasn't it always said before that the government always said there was no money in the national treasury? Manong Hussin Amin knows this; we were together in Congress during that time. Now, we are proving: if the funds are not stolen, they will not be insufficient.

It is clear: We are focusing on the welfare of the majority of our countrymen, and promoting initiatives that deliver lasting benefits. Projects that used to go through politics are now aligned with the proper process; benefits that used to be selective are now being expanded to include everyone. Even politicians who are not from our party are posting on social media, passing resolutions, or releasing ads to thank our administration for the new roads, bridges, other infrastructure, and other assistance in their respective areas. [Applause] It's clear: Politics is not the basis of our projects. In any province or municipality, regardless of the mayor's surname or party, we are now striving to deliver immediate benefits. With all that we have achieved, can anyone say they have been neglected?

For us, the measure of good leadership is simple. If the problems we inherited have been reduced, or completely solved, we can say that we have succeeded. If the exact same problems remain, or the problems passed on to the next generation have even increased, then it is clear that we have failed.

That is why I expect the help of our colleagues in the local government units, to ensure that what we are planting now will be nurtured in the coming times, and will not be cut short by those who want to return to the crooked path. You can count on me: If legitimate problems arise that you cannot address, the national government is here, especially me, as the father of the nation, to take steps to help all of you. [Applause]

Indeed, if we continue on the straight path, our destination will become even better. Think about it: If even now there are so many new roads, bridges, airports and ports bringing opportunities to your municipalities, what if we accelerate even more? If our economy is already growing now, how many more businesses will be established, how many more jobs will be created, if we ensure that integrity and honesty, the source of confidence, continue to be the pillars of governance? If even now, almost five years after restoring the hope of our Bosses, we have come so far, how much higher can we reach the dreams we can achieve if we continue and more reforms are implemented by the next leader?

The challenge before us now: Ensure that the fruits of our efforts become permanent. If we believe that the path we are taking now delivers benefits, it is only reasonable that we continue it. As leaders, we are called upon at every moment of governance: Let us be true to our word, choose what is right, and prioritize others. Let us bequeath to future generations a future that is far better than the one we inherited.

And in response to what Honorable Sandy Javier is requesting – although I just heard his suggestion now – we probably already have an answer that we should share with you. He reminded us that you can raise local taxes, but let's not talk about that especially since the elections are near, or many people might get angry with you. [Laughter] But because our economy has improved, our tax collection has also improved. Actually, regarding the pie that we are talking about, or a greater slice of the pie, here’s what happened: In 2010, the share from the IRA was P265.8 billion. This 2015, it is estimated to be P389.86 billion, excluding the Performance Challenge Fund and the Bottom Up Budgeting that we discussed. Before this request to increase the slice even came up, it seems the slice has already grown even without us slicing the pie again. [Applause]

You know, let me give you an example of my problem. You might notice that my hair is growing more and more. In our uniformed ranks, we spend a lot on them, especially on retirement and pension. Perhaps two years ago, it was already over P50 billion. Next year, it is estimated to be P80 billion for payment. The problem with that is twofold: First, there is really no pension system for our uniformed ranks, it all comes from the budget. Second, there was what is called “indexing.” When the salary of those who are active increases, the benefits of retirees also increase. You all understand, we have an obligation, but we have no source of funds. And we need to fix the system.

We are studying how to correct the system. The funds needed to establish their pension system – is almost two trillion pesos. Let me repeat, one estimate is 4.3 trillion; another estimate is 1.7 trillion. Our budget is 2.6 trillion. If we put in the entire budget, it’s still not enough. The bottom line is, I want to say yes. Sure, let's increase the slice of the pie. Perhaps I can commit to you, to those who are showing off, it’s not difficult to increase your funds. For the many problems that are causing us trouble, we are a bit cautious about increasing funds. It’s up to you. Let's work together here. Success will breed success. Excuse me, failure will breed an orphan. Let me repeat, we see and feel, we see the problems you are facing, our role is to reduce the problems you are facing now. Rely on that.

Good day. Thank you very much to all of you.
