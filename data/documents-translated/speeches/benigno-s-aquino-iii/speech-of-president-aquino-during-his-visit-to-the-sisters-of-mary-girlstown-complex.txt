Speech of the Honorable Benigno S. Aquino III, President of the Philippines, During his visit to the Sisters of Mary Girlstown Complex

Thank you very much. Let us all be seated.

Secretary, Brother Armin Luistro; Secretary Joel Villanueva; Secretary Rene Almendras; Congressman Roy Loyola; other members of the House of Representatives present, namely, Rep. Boy Blue Abaya and Rep. AA Advincula; of course Manong Ayong Maliksi, the longstanding governor and public servant and role model in Cavite; Sister Elena Belarmino; Mrs. Marixi Rufino-Prieto and other benefactors of the Sisters of Mary Boystown and Girlstown; alumni, teachers, and students of the Sisters of Mary Boystown and Girlstown; fellow workers in government; honored guests; my beloved countrymen:

Good afternoon to all of you once again.

While I was watching your singing and your actions, of course we were very impressed that you were so synchronized—no one was left behind, no one was ahead, no one cared [laughter]. Then, there are three members of the cabinet. I said, “Should I challenge these three to do it together?” Do you think they can do it while the music is playing? Maybe they can; isn’t there someone conducting? What if there’s no music anymore? Because earlier, the music had already stopped, yet you were still synchronized.

Perhaps the reason I’m emphasizing this is—especially in our lives in politics, there’s so much content to become discordant. If you all agree, “Oh, isn’t it good to do this here?” Let’s say “White,” there will always be someone who will object, saying, “No. It should be black.” Why? “Just because.”

There is something called, in any society, they call it a “contrarian.” What is a contrarian? No matter how good you are, no matter how well you do something, there will really be someone who opposes, because that’s what makes them happy—they need to oppose.

So earlier, while I was watching you, I said, “If we all really march together towards recognizing, understanding the needs of our brothers and sisters, our fellow human beings—if our lives are focused on our fellow human beings, why wouldn’t we reach where we want to go faster? Right?” Instead of everyone doing their own thing and being content—we have a choral group where everyone sings their own song. Of course, if we all sing different songs—can we even have a good choral group? We might also be good listeners because who will you listen to, 14 songs being sung? What clarity could there be in that?

Anyway, let's proceed to the formal portion of the speech.

When Sisters Maria and Elena invited me to visit the Sisters of Mary Girlstown, they asked me to give an inspirational message for you. This is what I can say after really seeing the teachers and students in this school: You are inspiring. Truly four years and five months, if my count is correct, that we have been serving the nation. Of course, I cover all the problems of the country, all Filipinos with problems, that is mine. Whether they are in the Philippines or not in the Philippines, that is my problem. We are humans after all, and somehow it is tiring. When everyone goes on vacation, you make sure their vacation is safe. When their vacation is over, and they return, that is also my job—to ensure their safe return.

My job is also to find jobs for everyone: to teach everyone to have the necessary skills to participate in the growth of our economy. And after that, there are really others who, maybe you heard my SONA, I said, breakfast is criticism; lunch is intrigue; dinner—all the ugliness is there. But I ended up here, Mrs. Prieto is right, if you are sad, come here, you will really be inspired. I, along with my cabinet, thank you all. It’s like the battery of a cellphone, we all got recharged when we’re here.

It is truly admirable that you all chose to continue your studies despite the challenges you face in life. You are so young, yet you carry such heavy burdens. You give hope, not only for yourselves, but also for your loved ones. You also bring hope to our country. When you graduate and begin to use the blessings you have received to be a light to others, you will become part of advancing a Philippines that is more progressive and closer to God. You prove that: when you give a Filipino an opportunity, regardless of his or her situation, no matter where he or she comes from, he or she will not waste it. Instead, he or she will do everything he or she can to contribute to the meaningful transformation of our society.

In 1986, Fr. Al Schwartz established the Sisters of Mary School in the Philippines. That was also the year when Filipinos ousted the dictator and chose my mother as the new leader. It was a new chapter in our history: a new path had to be laid after a long period of imprisonment in fear and abuse. The contribution of this school in guiding young people on the margins of society, who were beginning to find their place in a free country, cannot be denied.

During those times, I remember hearing a joke about my mother. It was said that she was like someone tasked to cook sinigang. She was only given water and salt as ingredients and told that it had to taste like sinigang. To those of you who are into culinary arts, can you do that? The dictator left behind a broken system, a collapsed economy, and a national consciousness that was truly wounded by the oppression of those in power.

That is what my mother found after EDSA. From a housewife, she became the President of the Philippines. And on her shoulders rested the responsibility of leading our people towards healing. Like you, she did not succumb to doubts. Instead, she bravely faced the challenges. It was clear to her: she was given a unique opportunity to be an instrument of change. She wholeheartedly devoted herself to serving the Lord and the Filipinos.

The trust of the citizenry and his deep spirituality were his weapons against trials. His way of life and leadership taught us that as long as you follow your conscience, and as long as the well-being of your fellow human being guides your journey, you will not lose your way. With this conviction, the previously impossible becomes possible.

I am pleased to observe that this belief and practice are imparted by the Sisters of Mary School to your students. I thank the administrators, teachers, and benefactors of this school for their solidarity in our endeavor to provide a better future for our youth, especially the poor. You can count on it: your government’s support for your good work will continue. [Applause] Support will continue, for example, from TESDA: From 2008 to 2013, 19 of your programs were registered and four were granted accreditation as assessment centers. Through this, we ensure that the training received by students in Technical Vocational Education and Training is sufficient and appropriate. Under the Training for Work Scholarship Program, 288 slots were granted to your students from 2010 to 2013. And I am sure Joel is looking [for a way] to increase that number. [Applause] Earlier, 200 scholarship vouchers were given to your students who will be part of TESDA’s Special Training for Employment Program. In this program, the specific needs of the community are identified, and then students are trained to meet them. With this system, graduates will not only be given employment; they will also be able to contribute to their community.

Through the cooperation of TESDA, one of your students won the Medallion of Excellence in Fashion Technology at the ASEAN Skills Competition held in Vietnam last October. She is on our right – Jessica Babon, of course, a credit to the Sisters of Mary, and to TESDA: I congratulate you on this success. You know, we haven't participated for seven years. And when we participated, there was a Medal of Excellence, several gold medals, several bronze medals. So next year, Joel promises us more medals. Unless we have the same hairstyle. [Laughter]

You have proven that we can go far when we walk together.

To the students here today, I will give you a warning: Life is not easy when you are outside of school, you all know that. I say this not to scare you, but to show the importance of humility and faith in God. Hold on to what Fr. Al often told you, and translated into Tagalog. He said this, “You were not created to be fat and small stepping stones in the mud, but to be eagles destined to fly high.”

The quality education and training you receive here at Sisters of Mary School is only the first step towards achieving your dreams. One day, some of you will become professionals and experts in various fields. Others will return to become part of the congregation, and will serve as guides for the next generation of students. Whatever path you choose, I am sure your teachers and the Sisters of Mary have prepared you for the challenges you will face.

Let us always remember: We do not only elevate ourselves when we achieve success. We also serve as inspiration to others, and contribute to their good deeds. Above all, our collective efforts will pave the way for the continuous positive transformation of our society. In other words, as we elevate our flight now, we ensure that the next generation will fly even higher. In this way, we further fulfill the Creator's plan for all of us.

You know – I just want to share with you what my father told me one day – we were talking about the importance of education. He said to me then, why are you famous today, but obsolete tomorrow? Why are you rich today, but poor tomorrow? If you take your studies seriously and get it into your head, everything said in your lessons, whatever happens, that is your wealth for life. While you have the opportunity, practice diligently so that when the time comes, whatever happens, you are ready.

And that is what we are trying to live by. You know, the difference between your generation and mine – of course, my father was among the first to be arrested during martial law – when we look at the towns that experienced martial law, they only gain freedom when there is a bloody revolution. And I didn't expect to surpass the point of skipping that bloody revolution. But as they say, maybe God has a plan for us and here we still are. I didn't think I'd reach 30. In just six years, I will be 60, it has doubled. Perhaps as long as I haven't finished God's plan for me here, we are still here.

Now, why am I sharing this with you? That was our destiny. We needed to go through those dark times. Perhaps we needed to feel, what does it mean to have no freedom? We needed to feel or see, what does it mean to have no hope? Now, we are changing all of this now.

Earlier, we came from Coca-Cola, the plant in Laguna, in Calamba. Before this plant was expanded, in 2010, Coca-Cola international promised to invest one billion dollars in us. They have already invested P1.2 billion. Their commitment – it was even ahead of schedule because it was supposed to be finished next year – it was brought forward, this year. Next year, it will be increased by half a billion. They employ eight thousand people directly. And there are others who sell their drinks, etc.

So, next year, we will begin what is called the demographic sweet spot, where many of our citizens are in the working age. Now, it is important if you are already at the age where you can work, you should have the corresponding skills to participate in the growth of our economy, right? If you have no knowledge to contribute, or you have not been given knowledge, and you lack the necessary skills, you will always be at the bottom.

So, that is our purpose. I cannot tell all of you that I guarantee that your results will always be good. I can help. We can open doors for you. My father also told me that. I can open doors for you. Whether they remain open is up to you. When I started working, he said he could introduce me, recommend me, he would be given a chance. But if I became unproductive in the office I entered, of course, I would be fired. That is what “being able to keep the doors open” means – it’s up to you.

So, what we are aiming for is to exhaust all opportunities that can be given to every Filipino so that no one is left behind. If you succeed, of course, that’s a partnership. That’s why earlier, when we were all together in our synchronized song number, it really showed that Filipinos can march in step, with one pace, right? My three cabinet members here might need to practice for two months to keep up with you. [Laughter] And of course, you are young. We are just a little older. The flexibility of your bodies and the flexibility of our bodies, yours is real, ours is a memory. But the determination to unite with you, so that when the time comes – I always say this – the time will come, we are human, we have a limit in this world. As we said when we were studying, there will come a point when the Lord will call us, and He will say, “Finished or not finished, pass your paper.” There is only one real ambition, before you are called by the Lord, you are already in the final stage of your life, in the last minute, I hope you can look back and say, “Wow, the changes I leave behind are much greater than what I found. And I was a part of that change towards goodness.” It’s always a choice. We can be selfish, or we can contribute together.

Just that last point, as far as I remember, my mother helped a lot here and visited you. Of course, I want to know how she really helped because, in this time, the election is really approaching, if you think about it, many people are looking for all the flaws: Your breathing is wrong, your combing of your hair is wrong, everything is wrong, right? You can see when you watch TV, I haven’t done anything right. But those who say that, it’s still the same. That’s okay. If you don’t want to join, stay behind, you have the freedom to stay behind. But we, who want to move forward and progress, please don’t obstruct us too much. So, we will find a way.

Just one last thought. The Americans have a saying, “Success has many fathers or mothers. Failure is a lonely orphan.” Here, in the past 28 years, a lot of success has happened. I think you have many adoptive fathers and mothers who will give the same opportunity to the many others who will follow in your footsteps. Please, let’s continue to help each other. Let’s continue the direction that the opportunity given to you, let’s make sure it is also given to future generations. And we, as much as we can, will strive to help you in this good endeavor and undertaking you are doing.

Good afternoon to all of you. Thank you very much.
