Speech of the Honorable Benigno S. Aquino III, President of the Philippines, at the Inauguration of the Iloilo Convention Center.

There are many reasons for our beloved Ilonggos to celebrate today. Earlier, we had the ceremonial launch of the Iloilo Business Park and the inauguration of the Richmonde Hotel. Now, we have launched your magnificent Iloilo Convention Center. These projects will serve as the springboard for the further development of Iloilo as a center of commerce and tourism in Western Visayas. The benefit it brings: Wider opportunities for many of our countrymen. The massive transformation of Iloilo is clearly visible: from a sluggish province before, it is truly progressing and ready to compete with other developed provinces like Cebu. It’s a bit sad, though, that with Iloilo’s progress now, when I return to Tarlac, they might say or ask me if I’m from Iloilo rather than Tarlac. [Laughter] My only answer will be that it’s hard to refuse the “Big Man of the Senate.” [Applause]

We are here at a structure that proves the positive results of good governance—the Iloilo Convention Center. This is truly something that the Ilonggos can be proud of. Aside from its design inspired by your Dinagyang and Paraw Festival, its world-class quality is also impressive. That is why we are doubling our efforts so that, before long, it will be known and be a source of pride throughout the Philippines.

We would like to express our gratitude, first of all, to Megaworld Corporation, led by Dr. Andrew Tan, for their great contribution to the realization of this project: Dr. Tan donated 1.7 hectares. [Applause] A big thank you also to our Big Man in the Senate, and your fellow Ilonggo—Senate President Franklin Drilon [applause] who is truly very enthusiastic about Iloilo and its neighboring provinces. Architect Coscolluela, I heard, also donated his services for the design of this structure. [Applause] Earlier, Senate President told me, “You know, the land was donated, free of charge, by Dr. Tan. The design, free of charge, by Architect Coscolluela. And Jose Mari Chan’s performances earlier were also free.” [Laughter and Applause] The only problem is that someone filed a case here, and even the case and insult were free. [Laughter] Let me clarify: This convention center is just one of the meaningful projects of our good Manong Frank. There’s also the Iloilo River Esplanade that you frequent, the Stage 2 of the Jalaur River Multi-Purpose Project that we broke ground on, as well as many roads, bridges, and infrastructure that he strived to fund and implement, including housing for our informal settlers. Truly: In the legacy he will leave in Iloilo, you will see the difference between the Straight Path and the Crooked Path.

This Iloilo Convention Center is a concrete example that, under the 'Daang Matuwid' (Straight Path), public funds go to the people. The cost of this project is P713.31 million. It aims to be a center for conferences and gatherings, not just in your city, province, and region, but also in neighboring regions. The good news is: it is scheduled to be used for large gatherings such as the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation until October. I heard that there are already confirmed bookings and inquiries here in the coming months until next year.

Mon Jimenez told me that there are 38 weeks remaining in our term, and currently, 30 are already reserved. Perhaps with a little more effort each month—perhaps even each week—we can fill this Convention Center. It hasn't even started operating, yet it's almost fully booked. We can already see, even now, that the benefits of this building are well worth the investment, and it is certain that it will provide even greater benefits.

This is the pillar of our infrastructure strategy in provinces like Iloilo. Through better roads and world-class structures, more tourists will be encouraged to visit. This tourism development directly creates jobs. And with a greater ability to improve themselves, citizens also contribute to the development of the economy and society. This is a cycle of empowering the country, the community, and our Bosses—the people.

In the past five years, we have seen the positive transformation of our country. We achieved this because we united. In 2010, we chose honest, upright, and reasonable governance. It is clear: this positive change we have achieved, you made it and continue to make it.

In the coming year, we will once again face a crossroads. I see this as a referendum on all the successes we have already enjoyed today. What is a referendum? The question is simple: Do you like what we have achieved during our term, or do you want to return to the old system where a few exploit the suffering of our countrymen? Do you think we have fulfilled or even surpassed the promised change when we were just starting? As far as I’m concerned, if the answer is yes, it is only right to continue what we have started and choose the one who will undoubtedly continue on the Straight Path. [Applause]

I am confident that, just as in 2010, the vast majority of our Ilonggo countrymen, if not all, will once again side with what is right and reasonable. We all know the champion of the Straight Path, none other than Mar Roxas [applause]—like you, also a son of Panay Island.

In the remaining time of our term, we will continue to push the limits of what we can do to alleviate the suffering of others, and to accelerate the benefits for the people. Every road and bridge we build, every business that opens and expands, every building we construct, we want to bring lasting relief to more Filipinos. We focus our programs not on the dictates of politics or whims, but where they are truly needed.

My challenge and call to each one of you: Now and in the years to come, let us continue and further enrich the reforms we have sown; and let us pass them on to the next generation with our heads held high.

I practiced a little Ilonggo again earlier. If it’s correct, it’s thanks to Jed Mabilog. If it’s wrong, it’s thanks to Jun Delantar [laughter]: Let us continue to cooperate towards the right path so that we can achieve the progress we have long dreamed of. [Applause]

My fellow citizens, I will repeat just for when we look at the Convention Center, let us remember one thing: This happened, many people helped; you who helped me in 2007 and 2010, Manong Frank who initiated it, Andrew Tan who donated the land, Architect Coscolluela who designed this building. We all helped, and we achieved something. Now you have the ability to really compete in the field of what is called MCE—meetings, conventions, exhibits—these activities. The vision, like that of Dr. Tan, saw a part of Metro Manila called Libis back then. I admit, I am old enough to have seen this place as grasslands. It was really remote. His concept: a township. Everything is there: the children’s school, the parents’ workplace, housing for everyone, even entertainment. That’s what is envisioned for our old airport here. It didn’t work out due to the needs, and perhaps in time, maybe even 5 years from now, you will complain to our officials, “Why is it so congested here? Why are there so many people here?” The point of this is: Our destination is in our hands. We have the freedom to decide where we want to go. Let us use that right decision, especially next year.

Good day. Thank you all very much.
