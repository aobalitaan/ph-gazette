Speech of the Honorable Benigno S. Aquino III, President of the Philippines, at the gathering with local government leaders of Cebu.

Let us all be seated.

I practiced earlier, I was going to have you all practice in front of me. But we are running out of time, it is no longer appropriate.

Of course, first of all, good morning to all of you. [Cheers] But I am also prepared if it becomes: Good noon. But there are still about eight minutes left.

This is a bit long. I need to read this. I apologize. Kapampangan is not easy to translate into Cebuano. So if there are any mistakes here, it's my fault. If this is correct, Agnes taught me how to say this properly, especially JunJun Davide.

During the campaign (is that right)… you all laughed. It seems wrong right away. During the campaign, I knew there were many beautiful women here in Cebu. But now, there are even more beautiful women here. [ Cheers ] Do you agree with that? Thank you very much.

Of course, I will first greet our governor, Agnes Magpale, and also thank her for her proper management—Cebu is peaceful, our countrymen here are taken care of, although there are other sectors who want to create drama and say that it's tense in Cebu. It seems that's not true.

Of course, we wouldn't be here if our provincial party chair, JunJun Davide, hadn't worked hard. I think I can guarantee to you how humble he is now, he might be even humbler after the election.

Secretary Mar Roxas; Secretary Jun Abaya; Secretary Edwin Lacierda; of course, Mayor Jonas Cortez; our congressmen, Luigi Quisumbing, Tommy Osmeña, and of course, Red Durano; our esteemed Senator Koko Pimentel; esteemed representative, Sonny Angara;

Our allies and companions on the straight and narrow path: led by Jun Magsaysay; Jamby Madrigal; Risa Hontiveros; honorable Mark Villar, representing his mother Cynthia; former Chief Justice Hilario “Jun” Davide; our strong and always able local government officials, allies from before to now and hopefully in the future; fellow workers in government; honored guests; my beloved countrymen; my dear relatives (we have more and more Cebuanos here):

We went here in Cebu, first of all, to inaugurate the Austal Philippine Shipbuilding in Balamban. This is their third shipyard in the world, and they chose to build it in the Philippines because of their trust in the diligence and skill of Filipinos. We attend many inaugurations like this, and each one is a symbol of the fruits of our efforts in the past years. It reflects the positive results of taking the straight and narrow path: the entry of businesses and the opening of jobs and livelihoods for Filipinos.

There is a big difference in the confidence they show now, compared to the fear and anxiety that prevailed when all they heard about our country was corruption. Therefore, the decision of the people to unite in our pledge that if there is no corruption, there will be no poverty is truly bearing fruit.

And I thought to myself: I can't visit Cebu without meeting with our party mates here, right? Because if not for the Cebuanos, we might not be president now—there might have been martial law and we would have been imprisoned. You remember, we were in the opposition back then. But because you trusted us, you gave us the opportunity to change our society.

I just want to convey this to you simply: every success of the government; all the good news that the nation receives—from forging peace agreements in Mindanao to the successive record highs in the stock exchange; from the influx of tourists and the successive endorsements of international magazines that truly say “It’s more fun in the Philippines” to the historic one trillion collection of the BIR—all of this, every member of the Liberal Party and our allies, including all of you, are part of these successes.

It feels like just yesterday when we chose to take the straight path, and in just two years and six months, we have already achieved so much. Despite this, there are still those who say that life in the Philippines is difficult—saying that the prices of goods are still high or that there are still not enough jobs. I don't question this. I just don't fully agree and we are not perfect. And we still have a lot to do together, and it is clear that we are still far from the finish line.

Perhaps what they forget to ask is: is there anyone who questions that we are moving forward in the right direction? Do they even think about where we came from and where we are now?

2013 is important for our country. In the coming May, elections will be held again; candidates will once again face the Filipino people. Behind the left and right speeches, the countless photo opportunities and handshakes, this is what I want to impress upon the minds of the citizens this election: The election, above all, is a crossroads where Filipinos will once again have the opportunity to decide.

The question: Will we continue to travel the straight path of progress? Or do we want to make a U-turn back to the corner of corruption and the road of poverty?

I keep saying this: our journey will only be smooth if we have allies who will not only go against the flow on the straight path but will truly work together to push our reforms.

With us now are some of our candidates for Senator; public servants whom I personally chose and trust. I am telling you myself: we need them to continue to move the country forward.

They, like us, have chosen the righteous path of public service. We support them, because aside from being incorruptible, they have a clear understanding of what is right to fight for and what is wrong that should be rejected by society.

Don't we choose our public servants because we trust them to truly serve the Filipino people? Isn't it unfair that you, who are already in office, are the one violating the law, right? Whether you are a simple citizen or a government official, Liberal or from another party, the law should not change or be inconsistent. It is not acceptable for those in power to simply choose which laws they will follow. The law should be fair for everyone.

In the coming May, the Filipino people will once again hold the power to shape the future of the nation. It is once again in our hands where we will lead our only country.

It is true that we have already started a beautiful change, but it is important that we join hands to continue moving it forward, towards a brighter future for Filipinos.

Before I conclude: Of course, as long as people live, there are problems. Because when the time comes that we have no problems to face, that will be the problem—no challenge, right? What I can say is, perhaps it’s better to have a problem like: What should we do with the dollar and peso exchange rate? It’s also a good suggestion to think about, we can borrow [in] peso bonds, which are even oversubscribed. But the Central Bank is asking us if we can borrow dollars here in the Philippines so that we can somewhat reduce the excessive volatility of the exchange rate. Right? It’s better to have a problem choosing where to borrow than to have a problem where no one will lend to us. It’s good to have a problem when we are mentioned as being stuck in traffic on EDSA. That’s right. Because there are many people on the road and our economy is very much alive. Rather than no traffic on EDSA because no one can afford gasoline to run their vehicles.

Now, the elections are coming, notice, now, I haven’t heard of any wrongdoing on our part here or there. They can’t say that the Conditional Cash Transfer is wrong, where we are helping 3.8 million families this year to keep children in school and to have the ability to improve their lives. Usually [during] elections, when someone is not in power, they will say that so-and-so is bad, so-and-so is unclear, the policy is wrong. Is that right? We had a suggestion here. Can we have a super coalition? They will all join us. I said, “Thank you. You’re joining us. I hope you’ll truly commit to joining us in walking the straight and narrow path.”

I would just like to ask: We can speed up the change in society, but we are a democracy. That’s why it is said, “Our vote will shape the future.” If we vote because someone sings well, I don’t know if their song will take us to where we want to go. If we are listening to this, “It’s embarrassing to my friend,” even if their policy is contrary to what he wants. We should really be able to face ourselves and believe that everyone we choose will accompany us on our journey on the straight and narrow path because the welfare of the next generation, we will shape it not tomorrow. We will shape it now, at the time of the election, and if I may appeal to you—as thin as my hair is, my face doesn’t get thicker, but I will also ask you: let’s work together to speed it up. That will happen, maybe it can be more than just a vote. Let’s also help those who deserve to be placed in power. All the changes we have made are in your hands because of you. We can speed it up even more, again, because of you. So to everyone, when we are praised and these successes are said, I always say, “You did this.” So let’s continue what we have done. Let’s intensify our efforts to truly change our society and our country for the good of all our countrymen.

Good day. Thank you all, and although no one invited me, Viva Pit Señor.
