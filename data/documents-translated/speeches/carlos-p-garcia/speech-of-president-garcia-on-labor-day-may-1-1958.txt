Secretary Castaño, distinguished guests, great workers of our country:

Today, on the first day of May, the entire Filipino nation pays tribute to the honor of our workers who are the true pillars of unity and strength of our country.

As President of this nation, I will gladly accept all criticism against me, just to give sincere happiness and a good future to all workers in our country.

It is truly astonishing that those who have been eased in life are the ones who have many complaints or say things to the government, while the poor and oppressed in life are the ones who endure to give the government a sufficient opportunity to organize our livelihood. If we examine the lifestyle of these successful men, they are the ones who do nothing to help the situation of the workers in our country.

I was the first to tell the country truthfully that our way of life is precarious now. I know that I will be criticized for that confession. But I mustered the courage to confess so that we can all help each other to steer our country through the path of hardship. Incidentally, fellow countrymen, this hardship we are experiencing is not only in our country. It is being experienced by the whole world now. Even the richest countries are feeling this hardship in their lives. What more the Philippines, a small country and newly independent?

Discovering a way to cure our problems is not obtained through anger. Nor is it through hasty methods. Slow but sure is certainly better than fast if it is unreliable.

If there is anyone we should thank now, it is none other than the workers of our country. They are the most oppressed, but they continue to help the government; they lack comfort, yet they know how to look after the good of the country.

More than anything, it is important to me that our workers, who comprise the majority of our nation, be given a larger share of life's blessings and that each one of them be granted a prosperous and stable livelihood. This is what dominates my heart.

In this instance, I would like to thank the workers of our nation for all the help they give to our government. I am pleased to confess to you that other countries, including America, have wondered why, in the midst of the hardship we are currently experiencing, we have no sensitive riots or strikes, something that has not happened in any other country. This is a sign of the good character of our workers: with them, nothing is unbearable if it is for the good of the Nation.

I also want to thank you for defending my humble self when I was criticized by a newspaper from a country that is close to our hearts.

I cannot forget all of this, and I will strive to reciprocate with a service that is honest and pure.

I hope that the light of the torches you carry in the parade today will also be the light that illuminates the path to a beautiful future for our nation.

Thank you all very much!
