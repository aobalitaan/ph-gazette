Speech of the Honorable Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo, President of the Philippines, to the Filipino community in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia

I am so happy to be with all of you… I am very happy to be with you, my fellow provincials…I am overjoyed to be with all of you, I remember the stories of my mother…I am happy to be with all of you, my siblings from the Visayas and Mindanao…I am grateful to be with all of you.

I am delighted that we are together, my beloved countrymen. I am very impressed by your unwavering concern for your family, work, and country.

Last year, 2008, was very historic. The turmoil of the world economy spread and put the well-being of billions around the world at risk.

I probably don't need to tell you this: Here in Saudi Arabia, is the largest Filipino expatriate community in the world. I want to assure you that your jobs here are stable, despite the global economic crisis because there are fellow countrymen losing jobs in various countries. But I want to report that there are more new jobs being offered. That's why remittances are still increasing by 13%. Thank you very much.

Meanwhile, in our country, we are continuing our program to create more jobs. So, as I always say, may the day come when working abroad is just a career option, and not the only choice for the hardworking Filipino. One of the things we are doing is creating retraining programs in the Philippines such as training in call centers, welding, baking and chef training, and others, which can prepare returning expatriates to work with us. We are also bringing professional licensure examinations to select locations where there are many expat workers such as Qatar, Hong Kong and now in Saudi Arabia to prepare you for more stable jobs and higher salaries. For example, for those who have graduated with engineering but do not yet have a license, our Professional Regulation Commission will bring professional exams here in Saudi Arabia. So, if you pass, you can be promoted and get a raise and your position will be more secure.

During this time of trial for expatriates, your government is not sleeping.

For government, it is now payback time. The heroic efforts of the expatriate workers will not go unreciprocated.

A program of full reciprocity shall be carried out to help those who lost their jobs.

The DOLE and the OWWA will lead the implementation of this program for you, as well as for the workers in our export industries.

The first phase of this is the Filipino Expatriate Livelihood Support Fund amounting to P1 billion which will be funded by OWWA and supported by government lending institutions such as the Development Bank Of The Philippines and Land Bank. Expatriates who have lost their jobs may use this fund to invest in new businesses or fund further studies or training.

Secretary Roque of DOLE is here. I have instructed him to ensure that the regulations and processes to access this fund are simple and not complicated. Let us not further burden returning expatriates with a lot of red tape.

The DOLE and OWWA were instructed to coordinate with government economic and investment agencies such as the Department of Trade and Industry and the Department of Agriculture – here are Secretary Peter Favila and Secretary Artie Yap – to identify new businesses that returnees can venture into.

The second component of the government’s comprehensive support is assisting returning expatriates in finding employment.

The DOLE-OWWA is establishing help desks in each province. This is so that you can be assisted and jobs can be matched with your skills, training, and experience. We match the jobseeker with the job.

There is still more assistance available. This is online information. Its function is about vacant jobs, like classified ads, “wanted: Carpenter” or “wanted: Welder” or “wanted: Plumber.” There are now about a hundred touch screen kiosks for this nationwide. It can now be accessed online at phil-job.net.

For returning expatriates who wish to work abroad and whose skills and talents are needed, the POEA is currently conducting a large-scale opening of employment opportunities in many countries. We are conducting a marketing blitz.

We are able to fund all these programs because, compared to many other countries, the Philippines remains an island of stability in an otherwise stormy global sea of economic troubles. It’s not just me saying this, but financial experts such as Standard and Poor’s. We transformed our economy several years ago. This is helping us now. As a result of dramatic reforms in collection and the creation of a broader tax base, expenditure monitoring, and banking reform, our economy has grown.

We are making transformative investments in the people, such as education, training, health care, social services, as well as infrastructure. We are investing in modernizing infrastructure like roads and bridges on an unprecedented scale. Do you know that it now only takes half an hour from Monumento to Clark when you take the North Luzon and Clark Expressway? The next time you go to the Philippines, you can drive from Aparri to Jolo using our roll-on-roll-off nautical highway system. Or you can vacation in our various world-class resorts such as Boracay, Cebu, Bohol and Palawan.

Among us, according to surveys, nine out of ten look forward with hope to the new year. It is very important that we all work together. Let us continue to help each other in all ways that will benefit our country.

Thank you very much.
