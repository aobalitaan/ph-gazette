What is at stake, and also included in what is at stake, is the continued reform of our economy, the cleaning of the environment, the improvement of hospitals, investment in education, and the creation of good jobs.

I hope you will inform or express or repeat that “reforms build; revolt destroys.” And because reforms build, that’s why I don’t lose focus on my work.

Through hard-fought economic reform battles that we have led with the help of some courageous members of Congress, the Philippine economy has finally broken loose from its historic legacy of lethargy.

New revenues, better collection, and a crackdown on tax cheats along with fiscal discipline has put the Philippines on a path to sustainable economic growth and stability. This is now being noticed by the world. Recently, Texas Instruments announced a one billion dollar investment to further expand their operations in the Philippines.

The Philippines today offers one of the best values in Asia for domestic and foreign investors.

And as investments increase, both from foreigners and Filipinos, of course their plants and offices will require the services of a security guard agency.

Our economic reforms are at the forefront of our campaign against poverty. Poverty reduction is our main objective. It will remain the focus of my remaining years in office.

Bringing down the budget deficit, which we have been able to do, is just the first step. In the coming three years, we will deliver to the people the tangible benefits from our reforms.

Reforms can only be built when we are able to invest in our people and infrastructure. That investment in our people includes helping to make it easier for training schools to conduct training for our security guards. That is part of our investment in our people. And alongside that, we will further strengthen investment in our country and people in order to:

Number one. To improve infrastructure to attract businesses to grow and employ more, because more jobs means less poverty. For example, the roads we are fixing, the north expressway leading to Central Luzon, construction is ongoing and many businesses are being established in Clark. Texas Instruments will put in one billion dollars there, but Hanjin came first. They have already started their one billion dollar investment, about 300 million dollars of their one billion dollars is already in Subic. I remember when I went to Hanjin in Subic, well, they have already employed about ten thousand welders. And that’s not finished because their one billion dollar investment is not yet complete. I asked the welders, “what was your livelihood before?” They used to be marginal fishermen. They barely caught enough fish to feed their families, with only a little left to sell. And as welders, they have a big job and a big salary. More jobs means less poverty.

Our second program for the next three years is to improve social services such as health insurance subsidies for indigent families and food-for-school programs. I don’t know if security guards have Philhealth. Because it varies… doesn’t it vary in employment arrangements? There’s salaried employment. With salaried employment there’s… it includes SSS, health insurance is included there. There are others, it’s contractual. So if it’s contractual, they don’t necessarily have SSS. That’s what we aspire to, especially for poor security guards. And I know many poor security guards in areas where there are many poor people. When I ask, what is your wife’s livelihood, or your husband’s, for example, many of them say security guard. Those poor security guards who usually just have extra work, we also want to give them Philhealth or health insurance if they are not covered by SSS.

And some of our security guards also have children studying in public school. And I don't know if they were informed that now, in our public schools throughout NCR, we are providing food-for-school. The children, one child per family, receives one kilogram of rice each day they attend school so that poor families can eat.

Our third program. Enhance support for education as a weapon against poverty through better buildings, new books and teaching materials, and livelihood courses like what our young security guard mentioned, the ladderized training for security guards that can lead to a Bachelor of Science in Criminology degree.

You know how many… How many units are needed to teach Bachelor of Science in Criminology? Because… But I learned now – I already knew there was a ladderized program – but I learned now that 39 units are needed for credit in Bachelor of Science in Criminology. That’s a large amount of study that can be removed. And if you have a degree in criminology, well, you can also join the Philippine National Police.

Because the law of the Philippine National Police requires a college graduate. And the Philippine National Police, because it’s large… Our budget has increased due to our tax reforms, so it is hiring new policemen. Therefore, training must continue so that they can continue as a security agency or, if they finish their… College degree in criminology because of the ladderized training, then many more will be able to enter, fully prepared for the job of a police officer.

This ladderized training, Mrs. Tablang actually went to Malacañang in May… No, April 30, because we were celebrating the ladderized training, ladderized education. And there is an agreement between the Philippine National Police and Angeles University. So the learning of our trainees in your training schools, 39 hours of that will be credited by Angeles University towards its Bachelor of Science in Criminology course. And we expect that if this is successful, many other universities will enter into agreements with the Philippine National Police.

Fourth, our program is cutting red tape and continuing to reduce production in the system.
