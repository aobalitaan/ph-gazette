Chairman of CHED, Dr. Garcia; our cabinet officials — Secretary Lorenzo, Secretary Diaz, Secretary Valisno; the President Emeritus of CLSU, former President of the University, President Battad; and the various CLSU Board of Regents, Administration and Faculty; and other officials of the number one rice-producing province — led by Governor Tommy Joson; one Outstanding Alumnus of CLSU, Mayor Nestor Alvarez; his congressman, Congressman Violago; the Chairperson of PCSO, Honeygirl Singson, who is also with me today; our graduates — graduating students, actually. I am happy to be with the CLSU community again — after having been here many times — especially now that our country is facing two great challenges: one is the poverty of many of our people; and the second is the unavoidable globalization.

We need to emphasize the very sensitive need for the development of agriculture and the countryside as our response to these challenges because it will affect a large part of our rural communities that are still below the poverty line. The job is to raise the level of productivity and efficiency of our agricultural sector so that we can achieve food sufficiency and be able to compete for our exported products. Only in this way can we strengthen our market economy in the countryside, and first, improve the economic condition of the majority of our people who depend on agriculture. And second, overcome the pressure of globalization, whether we like it or not, it is already here.

The challenges and tasks I have identified are the reason why my administration has given, is giving, great attention and energy to job creation in the agriculture and community sectors, along with the modernization of agriculture guided by social equity, based on social equity starting with the basic food sectors.

I am pleased that in a short period of time we are already achieving important inroads such as certified seeds, hybrid rice, job fairs, but there is a lot of work to be done, and the government cannot do this work alone. The national government cannot do this work alone. That is why I thank CLSU for taking the first steps to support the programs we have started.

In our fight against poverty, the first need is to have jobs. That is why I congratulate CLSU because since my birthday on April 5th until today, CLSU has hosted job fairs. As I understand it, our graduates today number 793, but the job fair has generated jobs – 877. So, that means if our graduates accept the jobs offered to you at the job fair, all of you will have jobs. Zero unemployment rate for the graduating students of CLSU.

In our fight against poverty, the second need – after jobs – is the modernization of agriculture based on social equity because the majority of our poor are in the countryside. I am also pleased with the cooperation of CLSU with PhilRice to become a center for hybrid rice production. This will start in May for one hundred and fifty hectares of CLSU land. This hectarage to be used by CLSU for hybrid rice production will earn 50,000 pesos per hectare or 7.5 million pesos per cropping or 15 million pesos per year, which will add to the financial needs of CLSU and create a thousand jobs here. This is a model of self-reliance and an example for other state colleges and universities, so I am directing CHED Chair Ester Garcia to give instructions to all state colleges and state universities, tell the other state colleges and universities to use their land grants to emulate the good example of CLSU.

Truly, the call is for a collective effort from all sectors — government, industry, academia and youth, you, the graduates of 2002. All of us should rally around the great work to rebuild our economy and country. Especially, because CLSU is a leading institution in agricultural education in our country. The call is for you graduates to dedicate yourselves to rebuilding the strength of our agricultural and community sectors.

We also understand, yes, you need to pursue your careers with the main goal of having a secure job for you and your families. But as Filipinos, I ask of you, I need you graduating students to give special consideration to entering career fields that will contribute especially to the agricultural and community sectors.

You graduates are fortunate to have had the privilege of receiving education from a leading institution like CLSU, which is well-known for its level of excellence, especially in agriculture and agricultural technology.

CLSU is recognized as a center of excellence in agricultural and fisheries education by CHED. Graduates of CLSU have a reputation for becoming topnotchers in the Agricultural Engineering Board examination. Graduates of CLSU also become topnotchers in the licensure examinations for Veterinary Science and Medicine. You were winners in 1998, the year 2000 and 2001.

The students of CLSU have already won many awards such as Ten Outstanding Students of the Philippines and Ten Outstanding Marketing Students.

The CLSU Rodeo Team has already won twenty-seven championships in rodeo competitions throughout various parts of the country in your thirty-four outings since 1984.

And CLSU has created many leaders in education from its graduates and faculty, especially the sixteen who became presidents of state universities and colleges, all from CLSU, and including Dr. Undan of CLSU.

CLSU has also created other distinguished leaders in various fields, as evidenced by three TOYM awards, three Outstanding Engineers, three Outstanding Women in the Nation Service, one PANTAS award from PCARRD as Outstanding Administrator – that was Dr. Battad, I think, during his time, six awards in Fisheries Research, one award in Animal Science Research, one award in Agricultural Journalism. So CLSU is really great. And I’m very proud that my family is a part of CLSU.

CLSU evokes memories of my father. It was during his time that this institution was elevated from a college to a university. Like many of the students and graduates of CLSU, my father was born in a nipa hut in a rural barrio in Central Luzon. He descended from a humble tenant who tilled three-fourths of a hectare of rice land that did not belong to them. My father, when he was in elementary school, suffered real hunger and deprivation as a child. He was the only man with truly peasant origins to rise to the position of presidency, and I am very proud of this.

To achieve the goal of addressing poverty, a poverty that he experienced first hand in his childhood, my father resolved to bring the country into a new era through a number of simple but meaningful missions. Among these were the attainment of self-sufficiency in the staple food of our people, mainly rice and corn. He likewise launched a bold but well-formulated socioeconomic program, the first medium-term development plan of our country, to put our country on the road to prosperity.

To a large extent, the conditions of his time helped the conversion of Central Luzon Agricultural College to a university. This in turn spurred his programs of social amelioration which are indelibly etched in the socioeconomic landscape of this region.

Today, CLSU has become a leading state university in agricultural education, engineering, veterinary medicine, agribusiness and entrepreneurship, as well as in environmental and ecological conservation. You have a campus that harbors various state-of-the-art research and development facilities. It is a wellspring of knowledge and social responsibility.

Speaking of social responsibility, beyond the world of academia, I would like to draw attention to the importance of the field of applied research and technology. The productivity and efficiency of our farmers are far behind our neighbors in Asia, China, Vietnam, Malaysia. One of the basic ways we can catch up is by introducing and training our small farmers in newer technologies. I am very proud to emphasize that CLSU is continuously building its development of applied technologies and its program that connects with global sources of various technologies that can be used in the Philippines.

For example, CLSU’s relationship with China, which is our source of rice technology. Not only hybrid rice but also rice agricultural machinery. Its relationship with Israel, which is a source of greenhouse technology.

And, as an adviser in the Metrobank foundation, I know that your faculty members are formidable. Some have been selected as Metrobank’s most outstanding teachers. These teachers from CLSU have consistently carried the torch of effective and durable mentoring.

Your president himself was a recipient of such national awards as most outstanding agricultural engineer, most outstanding research administrator, the one holding the most outstanding ASEAN engineering achievement. You are blessed with leadership and excellence.

With all these, you deserve unremitting support. And so, I’m indeed very grateful that you are all here today. Today, I am proclaiming a 20-hectare area in the CLSU reservation to be used for employee housing and construction of dormitories for students. This is part of the continuing effort to strengthen the institutional and human resources of your university. This is indispensable to the sustained development and progress of your region.

Let us fight poverty together. But we cannot rush into battle without weapons. I believe that the key to peaceful change is the rise of a new love for the education of the new generation.

I firmly believe that expanding education will be the main weapon towards a higher standard of living, particularly by giving attention to it in the sciences along with agricultural science and engineering.

Currently, the Philippines is the home of the great Filipino worker, and with him is the great farmer worker. We truly have a competitive advantage in our human resource skills-intensive service sectors. Let us use the knowledge of our service sectors – math, science, engineering, information technology – to make our farmer even greater. According to various studies, the great Filipino worker leads as knowledge workers in the world. No one can defeat the great Filipino worker when it comes to efficiency, dedication, and loyalty to work. The whole world knows this, and we are proud of it. Let us not leave behind giving the farmer the tools to achieve greatness and also be able to say he is among the great farmers of the world.

This should serve as our motivation to strive harder. Let us not be complacent. Let us not allow the world to lose its trust and admiration for the great Filipino worker. Let us use what we have learned here to prove that the Filipino farmer is also a great Filipino farmer who can face the challenges of the whole world. We must strengthen education for the future.

To my beloved graduates, amidst the dangers of the new war on terrorism and globalization, the remarkable rise of the agricultural sector should serve as inspiration for all of us to strive to achieve our dreams for our country.

Let us work together in a new EDSA, the new movement in the first decade of the twenty-first century, the EDSA that will suppress poverty.

I have great faith that through our tireless efforts and our continued prayers to the Almighty, we will achieve the desired development for every Filipino. Let us continue to strengthen and reinforce our unity so that we can achieve even more successes in the future.

Let us work together to develop our country, the home of the great Filipino worker which should also be the home of the great working farmer. So to all of you graduating students, congratulations! And I want to tell you, I need you so that our agriculture can progress, our country can progress, and we can eradicate the poverty of our country.

Thank you very much and good luck to all of you.
