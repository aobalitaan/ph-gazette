Thank you to the officials and citizens of Nagbalayong in Morong, Bataan. Your concern for our turtles and the establishment of the Bantay Pawikan People’s Organization are commendable. This is a great help to the Pawikan Conservation Project.

We released the young turtles into the sea earlier. They will have a better chance of survival there, and we can be more assured of our conservation efforts. And in the long run, Filipinos now and in the future will benefit from the wealth of the ocean.

We also demonstrate our appreciation for the environment, which is now a pillar of our development framework.

Yesterday we celebrated an important step in caring for our nature with the signing of our national budget.

Our budget reflects our values and policy priorities for investing in our people. We are allocating funds to things that will further advance our country. This is what we call the “Three E’s” – Economy, Education and Environment.

Every Filipino wants a good job, food on the table, good education for their children and a healthy and clean environment with clean air, clean water and clean land.

Today, we celebrate the Environment in Bataan. Filipinos love our country. We must be careful as stewards of our natural environment. The environment is a gift from God. We should not abuse the creation of God. This includes a commitment to clean air, clean water, and a clean and healthy environment, free from toxic waste that could harm each of us.

In the Environment, we will focus on:

Conservation – We need to beautify our villages and cities and rid them of trash; build more urban parks and recreation areas; purify our water and clean up industrial sites that are both unhealthy and aesthetically unpleasant. From our newly-approved budget that is allocating the biggest chunk to social services, we have put aside P 5 Billion for conservation activities from the DENR’s P 8 Billion total budget.

Energy independence – We need to tap alternative indigenous sources of power and energy, and we put a premium on green and renewable energy. We are veering away from imported fossil fuels whose prices are not only sky high, but also highly volatile. Our budget gives P274.16 million to support DOE’s efforts on energy resource development and utilization and conservation activities. The PNOC has its own billion of corporate funds for the development of New and Renewable Energy sources even as we work harder to convince investors to venture into the promising areas of solar, wind, geothermal and biofuel energy.

Reforestation – We will recover our lost forests through massive tree planting with the help of communities. The DENR budget sets aside P 300 million for reforestation. We have also convinced several countries like New Zealand to give their grants as reforestation projects. We have also instructed PNOC to spend on forestation to recover the oxygen consumed by oil, which causes global warming.

The protection of our national parks and wildlife refuges is also high in our priority. We want to turn our coral reefs and beaches, the rice terraces — our contribution to the wonders of the world — as well as our mountain vistas and tropical forests into national parks and international sanctuaries for eco-tourism and scientific research. P 240 Million will be spent for protected areas, apart from what they can raise as gate receipts from holidaymakers. There are specific provisions for wildlife sanctuaries, including P 5 million for the Pawikan Conservation Project. I am also instructing the Philippine Tourism Authority to use funds for this. Meanwhile, I brought a check as assistance for the livelihood of Sagip Pawikan to build lodging rooms for tourists and marine biologists at this center.

While we have already accomplished much, there is still much to be done. We plan to work diligently in the next two years until the end of my term in 2010 to fulfill our Philippine Reform Agenda. We need to and will, push for more reforms. We will fight for the Economy, Education and the Environment.

“Take care of Nature – A Weapon Against Poverty” – this theme is fitting to our desire to combine education and the economy with the preservation of nature. Even the overflowing wealth of nature can easily be depleted if its use is reckless. But if it is properly cared for, we will be blessed with more from the sea, forests and everything around us, and from these resources, millions of citizens will rise from poverty now and future generations.

For example, before, many of you used to collect turtle eggs or catch turtles to sell, but now that you know the value of this creature, you are leading the way in conservation. And you have also proven that your livelihood will improve if you defend and help the turtle population grow instead of decimating it through relentless pursuit.

Nagbalayong is a good example of how conservation efforts can be turned into livelihood opportunities that can lift people up from poverty. Thank you very much for your good example.

I expect you to continue this. I hope that all Filipinos will emulate your dedication in taking care of the environment as a fight against poverty.
